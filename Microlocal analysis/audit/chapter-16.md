## Findings

1. **Lemma 16.2.5(ii) and the admissible-embedding definition, lines 452–463 and 567–581. The hypotheses permit a change in the index class.** The individual conditions on $J_bA_bJ_b^*$ and $J_b'A_bJ_b'^*$ do not imply smooth mixed terms $J_bA_bJ_b'^*$, which the rotation proof uses. There is a counterexample to the conclusion as written. Use the trivial point-fibre fibration over $B=S^2\times S^1$, the trivial line, and $g(b,z)=z^{-1}$. For one embedding take a constant unit vector in a fixed two-dimensional subspace of smooth sections of $Y$. For the other, at each $b$ choose any unit vector in the Hopf line $L_b\subset\mathbb C^2$, with no smoothness in the choice. It meets the stated admissibility conditions because conjugating scalar $A_b$ gives the smooth family $A_bP_{L_b}$. The two conjugated maps represent different classes. On the $S^1$ factor the families Toeplitz index gives $[1]$ and $[L]$ in $K^0(S^2)$, respectively. Require smooth dependence of $J_b$ on smooth families of sections with the relevant continuity in seminorms, or use a specified class of explicit embeddings for which all mixed kernels are smooth. The tubular embeddings constructed in the chapter can meet the stronger condition.
2. **§16.8, lines 1727–1758. The joint kernel prefactor and parameter description disagree with the scaled variables.** The displayed changes of variables multiply the right density by $\epsilon^{n+k}\delta^k$, with $n$ the fibre dimension and $k$ the base dimension. Use $\epsilon^{-n-k}\delta^{-k}$, as in the joint calculus of Chapter 6. With the printed factor and fixed $\delta>0$, a uniformly Schwartz profile gives an operator norm $O(\epsilon^{n+k})$, so the intended nonzero limit disappears. The additional $\delta$ squeezes the base directions in the displayed model. The wrong prefactor is also in Melrose's Eq. (12.64). This section sketches an independent adiabatic proof; the displayed proof in §16.7 uses Chapter 15's analytic multiplicativity.
3. **§16.1, lines 293–296. The leading symbol does not determine the support of all jets.** Take $A_\epsilon=\epsilon Q_\epsilon$, where $\sigma_{\rm sl}(Q)$ is supported over an open set disjoint from $K$ and nonzero there. The leading symbol of $A$ is zero. A perturbation flat in $\epsilon$ cannot erase the next jet there. Drop “modifying it by an element of $\Psi^\infty$” when choosing a fresh quantization, or assume the support condition on all the jets. The excision proof only needs a fresh choice.
4. **Remark on the semiclassical cotangent bundle, lines 407–417. The symbol of the same family is reparametrized by the inverse dilation.** For $\epsilon'=\epsilon T(\epsilon)$ the new symbol is $\sigma(x,\xi/T(0))$, as seen by writing $\epsilon D=\epsilon' D/T(\epsilon)$. Exercise 16.5's answer computes the different operation $\tilde A(\epsilon)=A(2\epsilon)$ correctly, but identifies it with this convention at lines 511–515. State both directions explicitly.
5. **§16.2, lines 430–435 and 478–486. Fix the support statement and radial cutoff.** $A(I+A)^{-1}A$ is supported in $K\times_B K$ for an enclosing compact $K$, and can have larger support than the kernel of $A$. For example two composable disjoint rank-one arrows produce a third arrow in $A^2$. Also choose $h(|v|^2)$ or take $h$ constant near zero in the tubular embedding so the radial profile is smooth. The general open-fibre setting does not supply a locally trivial bundle of groups without an extra hypothesis; the supported family construction works without such a trivialization.
6. **Remark 16.2.8 on relative projections and Exercise 16.3.** The Fredholm proof should use $PQ$ as a parametrix for $QP$, as the solution does. The hint's positive-rank model $P=Q+\Pi$ can only have nonnegative index. $P=0$ and $Q$ rank one already give index $-1$. Allow both a finite-dimensional addition and a removal, or use the trace proof in the solution. The solution's final parenthetical repeats the inadequate reduction.
7. **§16.5, lines 1160–1165.** The trace of the difference of idempotents computes the rank of the relative index. The individual ranks can both be infinite. Cite the precise loop trace calculation now proved in the solutions.
8. **§16.9, lines 1809–1819. The Todd-class calculation needs the rank normalization.** In the chapter's orientation the fibre integral of $\operatorname{Ch}(\lambda_\mathcal V)$ alone changes sign when a trivial real line is added. It equals $(-1)^{\operatorname{rank}\mathcal V}\operatorname{Td}(\mathcal V\otimes\mathbb C)^{-1}$ in Chapter 15. The ambient Bott normalization supplies the other sign, leaving the $(-1)^n$ in the index formula. Adding a line therefore preserves the normalized class. For the spectral-section remark the specific source is Melrose–Piazza's families theorem, and the definition should state the spectral cutoff condition.

9. **Definition 16.2.1 allows a finite-dimensional target, and Exercise 16.2(b) also needs an infinite-dimensional hypothesis.** If $Y$ is a point and $F$ is a line, the ambient smoothing group is $\mathbb C^\times$. For $B=S^3$, maps to this group are null-homotopic, whereas $K^{-1}(S^3)\cong\mathbb Z$. Use an infinite-dimensional target for the asserted classifying-space identification, or explicitly take the stabilization. In Exercise 16.2(a) the identity projections handle zero-dimensional fibres. For part (b) take the identity fibration of $S^2$ and a Hopf line for $E$. Its $L^2$ bundle is the same nontrivial line and has no global orthonormal frame. State part (b) for infinite-dimensional Hilbert fibres.
