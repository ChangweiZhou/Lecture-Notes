## Symbol convention settled (README item 4)

In Chapter 2, §2.9 (labels `euclidean:def:polyhomogeneous`, `euclidean:lem:ph-basic`, `euclidean:prop:ph-algebra`, `euclidean:rem:ph-orders`, `euclidean:prop:ph-symbol`, and the complex-order subsection):

1. **Classical (one-step polyhomogeneous) symbols.** $S^m_{\rm ph}$, $m\in\mathbb C$: these are the $a\in S^{\Re m}$ with $a\sim\sum_j a_{m-j}$ and $a_{m-j}$ homogeneous of degree $m-j$ in $\lvert\xi\rvert\ge1$. The degrees run over $m-\mathbb N_0$ only.
2. **Order structure.** $S^{m-k}_{\rm ph}\subset S^m_{\rm ph}$ and $\Psi^{m-k}_{\rm ph}\subset\Psi^m_{\rm ph}$ *only* for $k\in\mathbb N_0$. $\Psi^m_{\rm ph}\Psi^{m'}_{\rm ph}\subset\Psi^{m+m'}_{\rm ph}$ for all $m,m'\in\mathbb C$, and $(\Psi^m_{\rm ph})^*=\Psi^{\bar m}_{\rm ph}$. $\bigcup_m\Psi^m_{\rm ph}$ is **not** a vector space. The filtered $*$-algebra is $\Psi^{\mathbb Z}_{\rm ph}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb Z}\Psi^k_{\rm ph}$, and each coset $\bigcup_k\Psi^{\mu+k}_{\rm ph}$ is a filtered bimodule over it. "Classical of order $m$" means $A\in\Psi^m_{\rm ph}$, and the order is part of the data. Also $\Psi^m_{\rm ph}\subset\Psi^{m'}$ (general class) for every real $m'\ge\Re m$.
3. **Two symbol targets.**
   - General class $\Psi^m$: $\sigma_m:\Psi^m\to S^m/S^{m-1}$, which is exact with kernel $\Psi^{m-1}$ (Prop. `euclidean:prop:symbol-sequence`).
   - Classical class $\Psi^m_{\rm ph}$: $\sigma_m:\Psi^m_{\rm ph}\to S^{[m]}$, which is exact with kernel $\Psi^{m-1}_{\rm ph}$ (Prop. `euclidean:prop:ph-symbol`). Here $S^{[m]}(\mathbb R^p;\mathbb R^n)$ is the space of smooth functions on $\mathbb R^p\times(\mathbb R^n\setminus0)$, homogeneous of degree $m$ for all $t>0$, with $z$- and $\xi$-derivatives bounded on $\lvert\xi\rvert=1$. On a compact manifold this becomes $C^\infty_{[m]}(T^*X\setminus0)=C^\infty(S^*X;N_m)$.
   - Compatibility: the map $h\mapsto[\rho h]$ is an injection $S^{[m]}\hookrightarrow S^m/S^{m-1}$ that carries the classical symbol to the general one. It is not surjective ($\cos\log\langle\xi\rangle$). An operator in $\Psi^m$ has a homogeneous principal symbol representative iff it lies in $\Psi^m_{\rm ph}+\Psi^{m-1}$.
   - Any statement that assigns a *homogeneous* principal symbol, or that prescribes one, refers to the classical calculus. The text of Ch. 2 says this explicitly.
