The sentence there ("`scindex:thm:thom` is the statement that the fibrewise quantization map of [that remark] takes the Bott class … to 1") is now a comparison with a sketched map. It is not a dependency, because `scindex:thm:thom` has its own proof and the remark already notes that the $O(k)$ case has no patching problem. ## Report to the editor

- Fixed: 16 ledger rows (counting the three downgraded open rows and the two made-precise external rows) and 7 prose findings, including README item 1 and item 3 (Ch 13).
- Disputed: none. I verified every audit claim, including the $S^5$ counterexample, the $S^2$ lift obstruction, the spectral bound and the Mehler normalization.
- Irreparable: none. The three OPEN items were **downgraded to explicit sketches** rather than completed: the isotropic rotation argument, general staged quantization/Thm 13.10.9 for $n\ge2$ or non-compact $X$, and Fock descent for nontrivial bundles. Completing the staged quantization or Fock descent would need substantial new analysis. Nothing in the book depends on them as proved. In Chapter 13 itself, Theorem 13.10.9 and the quantization half of Theorem 13.11.3 are now restricted to $n=1$ (compact $X$, and $V=X\times\mathbb C$ respectively).
- Cross-file breakages: none. One optional wording suggestion for `scindex:rem:why-trivial` in 14b (see above).
- New labels: `index:rem:perturb-family-large-r` and `bott:rem:sl-iso-general`. No labels were removed or renamed.
- New references (plain text, for the editor to add to bibliography.tex if wanted): M. F. Atiyah and F. Hirzebruch, *Vector bundles and homogeneous spaces*, Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 3 (1961), 7–38, cited in Remark 12.4.4. R. Palais, *Homotopy theory of infinite dimensional manifolds*, Topology 5 (1966), 1–16, was already cited in plain text in the Ch 13 Notes; the citation now names the corollary to Theorem 15.
- The definition of smooth families in Ch 12 is now stronger: it requires uniform classical remainder estimates. Families built later by quantizing smooth classical symbol families satisfy it automatically. Other chapters that invoke "smooth families" in the sense of Ch 12 inherit this standard condition.
