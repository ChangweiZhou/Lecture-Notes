## Chapter 1. Tempered distributions and the Fourier transform

### Findings

- **Opening example, lines 6–8.** The Laplacian has a locally integrable fundamental solution in every positive Euclidean dimension. In dimensions 1 and 2 it is a multiple of $|x|$ and $\log |x|$, respectively, and in $n\ge3$ a multiple of $|x|^{2-n}$. The point about genuinely distributional fundamental solutions can use the three-dimensional wave equation instead. [Chapter 1 opening](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/01-distributions.tex#L6-L12)
- **Tensor order, lines 357–373.** The construction acts by $v$ on $x'$ and integrates the last variable. The general exterior-product convention on lines 296–298 denotes this as $v\boxtimes1$, although the first lemma calls it $1\boxtimes v$. The latter becomes the correctly ordered notation in Corollary 1.3.2, where the integrated coordinates come first. The operations and estimates themselves agree with their displayed integrals.
- **Scope of the kernel figure and quotient, lines 929–974.** The quotient $\mathcal S'/\mathcal S$ detects behavior at infinity as well as local singularities. For example, the smooth tempered distribution $1$ is nonzero in the quotient. The statement that a pseudodifferential kernel is no longer supported on the diagonal also needs “can extend off the diagonal.” Differential operators belong to the calculus.
- **Proposition 1.7.1, lines 1034–1048.** The criterion is correct. The example with $s\ne1$ includes $s=-1$, which gives an orthogonal map. For an invertible real scaling write $|s|\ne1$. The maps extend continuously to the one-point compactification, so use the $C^1$ qualifier when restating the failure.
- **Proposition 1.8.3 and Exercise 1.18, lines 1441–1458 and 1621–1624.** The proofs assume invertible linear blocks. A constant affine map preserves $V$ in the sense stated and cannot be a diffeomorphism on the compactification. Likewise the zero linear map preserves both summands in Exercise 1.18. Use “affine automorphism” and “linear automorphisms.” The smooth extension of the inclusion in Exercise 1.18 starts from the inclusion of each uncompactified summand. [Proposition 1.8.3](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/01-distributions.tex#L1441-L1454)
- **Remark 1.8.4, lines 1478–1489.** The two nonextension claims require $\dim U,\dim V>0$. With both factors present, $u=te$, $v=c$ give the same radial endpoint for every $c$, with different product endpoints. Conversely $u=te$, $v=td$ and $u=t^2e$, $v=td$ have the same product endpoint and different radial endpoints. With one zero factor the spaces are identical.
- **Exercise 1.19, lines 1630–1637.** The exercise asks for a diffeomorphism of one fixed collar $[0,\epsilon)\times S^{n-1}$. The coordinate change $z\mapsto 2z$ doubles the radial variable and precludes this. State the conclusion for germs of neighborhoods of the front face. The solution already proves this form.
- **Solution 1.18, lines 595–603.** Its formula on $F$ gives the whole sphere at infinity as image. At $a=0$ and $b=0$ it hits the two axial spheres. The complement of those axial spheres is the image of the relative interior $a,b>0$. [Solution 1.18](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/S-01-distributions.tex#L595-L603)
- **Solution 1.11, lines 317–330.** The sketch of the noncontinuous case leaves two steps implicit. On failure of the punctured-neighborhood estimate, each compact support has positive distance from $x_0$, so choose the next test in a smaller punctured neighborhood inside that distance. This yields disjoint annuli and gives the stated smooth, compactly supported gliding-hump sum. Once the estimate holds, all point evaluations $Pu(x)$ for $x\ne x_0$ in the neighborhood depend on jets through one common order, by inserting a cutoff around $x$ with support away from $x_0$. Compute their coefficients from fixed polynomial tests on the whole neighborhood. Those coefficient functions are smooth across $x_0$, and continuity of $Pu$ gives the formula at $x_0$. The continuous case of the solution is complete.

## Chapter 2. Pseudodifferential operators on Euclidean space

I read the full chapter and all 19 solutions. I checked the oscillatory integral, exact left reduction, composition, weighted Sobolev estimates, and holomorphic composition directly against the definitions and their proofs.

### Findings

- **Proposition 2.9.4, lines 1796–1825.** The one-step classical spaces over arbitrary real orders do not form the claimed filtered subalgebra. Both $\mathrm{Id}$ and $\langle D\rangle^{1/2}$ are one-step classical, but the symbol $1+\langle\xi\rangle^{1/2}$ has a leading degree $1/2$ and a nonzero degree-zero term. The allowed exponents of any one-step expansion differ by integers. In particular, for order $1/2$ the degree-zero term fails the first remainder estimate $S^{-1/2}$. Arbitrary real-order inclusions also fail, as $1\in S^0_{\mathrm{ph}}$ and $1\notin S^{1/2}_{\mathrm{ph}}$. The fixed-order linear spaces, the integer-shift inclusions and the product rule are sound. [Proposition 2.9.4](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/02-euclidean.tex#L1796-L1825)
- **Principal symbol of a differential operator, lines 1142–1147.** The displayed formula using only the terms $|\alpha|=m$ needs integer $m$. For $m=1/2$, the identity is an operator of order at most $m$ and has nonzero class $[1]\in S^{1/2}/S^{-1/2}$, although the displayed sum is empty. For real $m$, retain polynomial terms of degree strictly larger than $m-1$.
- **Principal-symbol language, lines 1131–1139 and 2724–2727.** For the chapter's general symbol class, the coordinate-invariant object is a class modulo one lower order. The identification with an actual homogeneous function is available in the classical case. A degree-zero symbol such as $\cos(\log\langle\xi\rangle)$, used in Exercise 2.9, shows that a general symbol need not have a homogeneous representative.
- **Proof of Proposition 2.9.5, lines 1852–1859.** The claim $|a_m|\ge c/2$ on the entire set $|\xi|\ge1-\delta$ fails when $m<0$. Use that bound in the annulus $1-\delta\le|\xi|\le1$ and retain $c|\xi|^m$ in $|\xi|\ge1$. These two bounds yield exactly the weighted reciprocal estimate used in the proof. The proposition survives.
- **Lemma 2.1.8, lines 330–336.** The cutoff equals zero on the ball of radius $C+1$. It need not vanish on a neighborhood of that entire closed ball. The proof can glue zero on $|\xi|<C+1$ to $\psi/a$ on $|\xi|>C$, where the formulas agree on the overlap. Lemma 2.1.11 also lists complex conjugation as an example of a linear map at lines 433–436. Its proof uses additivity, which suffices.
- **Opening and roadmap, lines 5–7 and 61–67.** The inverse of an elliptic order-zero differential operator can itself be differential, for example the identity. Specify a positive-order scalar differential operator for the illustration. The roadmap identifies residual operators with smooth kernels without the uniform rapid off-diagonal estimates required on Euclidean space. Proposition 2.3's residual criterion includes these estimates.
- **Square-root discussion, lines 1431–1436.** The positive representative is $C-|\sigma_L(A)|^2$ for the principal symbol class of $C-A^*A$. In general the full left symbol of $C-A^*A$ is not even real. The subsequent proof makes the correct distinction.
- **Solutions 2.6, 2.10, 2.12 and 2.17.** The Weyl check $x\xi$ on solution lines 396–398 falls outside the uniform calculus. Replace it by $f(x)\xi$ with $f$ smooth and bounded with all derivatives. The holomorphic and smooth Borel constructions use $K_0$ while their exhaustion starts at $K_1$, and the smooth construction quotes $\epsilon_{M-1}$ at $M=0$ for an empty sum. Start the exhaustion at zero or choose the first cutoff separately. In the affine solution lines 1129–1134, the annular support condition applies to positive-order derivatives of the cutoff. Bound the cutoff itself by its sup norm. These changes preserve the solutions.

### Findings
