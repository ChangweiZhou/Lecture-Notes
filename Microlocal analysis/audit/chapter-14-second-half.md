# Chapter 14, §§14.5–14.7 and Exercises 14.5–14.10

## Findings

1. **Proposition 14.5.5 asks for an impossible cutoff estimate.** A cutoff equal to one near zero leaves $\partial_\rho^j(\chi(\rho/\varepsilon_j)\rho^j b_j)|_{\rho=0}=j!b_j(0)$, regardless of $\varepsilon_j$. Even $b_j=1$ rules out the claimed $C^j$ estimate. Use $C^{j-1}$ and choose the sequence on the parameter exhaustion. To get the divisibility of each tail by $\rho^N$, use convergence in $C^\infty$ and the vanishing of its first $N$ jets; for the joint case apply this to each face and divide successively. The proposition survives. [Proof, lines 1117–1134](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/13-product.tex#L1117-L1134)

2. **Lemma 14.5.6 cannot drop compatibility and prescribe two independent restrictions.** If a nonzero datum at the corner is prescribed on one face, setting the other datum to zero cannot give both restrictions. The displayed joint extension works for compatible pairs. For a separate map, just extend that face in its own collar; the other face is free. The separate maps are surjective. [Proof, lines 1179–1195](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/13-product.tex#L1179-L1195)

3. **Proposition 14.6.8’s coordinate proof loses the part of the $Y$-symbol that is smoothing in the $Z$-normal variable.** The amplitude expansion determines an answer modulo $S_{\rm ph}^{M,-\infty}$. This remainder can have a nonzero front symbol, for example with $a=e^{-|\zeta|^2}\langle\eta\rangle^M$. To justify the exact transformation, take the front limit directly in the convergent Fourier-reduction integral from Lemma 14.6.2. For a fixed $(w,\zeta,\eta)$ with $\eta\ne0$, Peetre's bound after dividing by $t^M$ gives a polynomial bound in the Fourier integration variables, uniformly for $t\ge1$. The rapid transform of the amplitude dominates it. The limit is independent of the Fourier variable dual to $y$, so integration in that variable evaluates the amplitude at $y=0$. The remaining $\zeta$-integral is the exact fibre-normal reduction. This gives the printed transformation law and retains the smoothing part of the front symbol. [Proof, lines 1559–1585](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/13-product.tex#L1559-L1585)

4. **The base-symbol target and the three subalgebra inclusions must specify classical operators.** The manuscript's ordinary $\Psi^m$ notation includes nonclassical symbols, whereas every local product-type symbol in this chapter is weighted smooth. For instance, $\sin(\log\langle\zeta\rangle)$ is an ordinary order-zero symbol with no radial limit. A fibrewise multiplier with this local symbol gives a nonclassical order-zero operator. It cannot lie in the classical product-type calculus, and the constant family of such operators is outside the base-symbol range. The same issue applies to an ordinary symbol in all variables and to compression of a nonclassical base symbol. State the base target and Proposition 14.7.10 for the classical spaces, and propagate that convention to the Chapter 15 proof. Lemma 14.7.5(i), in contrast, legitimately uses nonclassical ordinary seminorms. [Target, lines 1784–1823](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/13-product.tex#L1784-L1823) · [Subalgebras, lines 2026–2045](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/13-product.tex#L2026-L2045)

5. **The compressed-operator proof identifies the full left symbol only up to an order drop of one in the base.** The actual kernel uses $e_j^*(z',y')$, so reducing to the left produces derivatives in $y$. For $P=D_y$, the first missing term is $f_iD_y e_j^*$, which is smoothing on each fibre but has a delta kernel in the base. The formula in the text therefore holds modulo $\Psi_{\rm pt}^{m-1,-\infty}$, and suffices for the base symbol. The density line in part (i) should also express cancellation as $\Omega_R(M)\otimes\Omega(N^*M_\phi^2)\cong\Omega_R(M/B)$ on the fibre product. [Proof, lines 2047–2075](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/13-product.tex#L2047-L2075)

6. **Remark 14.7.11 and Exercise 14.7 need positive fibre dimension.** With $f=0$, the main face is absent and the coordinate argument requiring $\omega\in S^{f-1}$ has no such point. Take $Z$ a point and a nonzero smoothing $P$ on $B$. The lift is then an ordinary smoothing operator, hence product-type, and $P$ need not be differential. For $f>0$, the derivative argument in the solution is sound. [Remark, lines 2078–2088](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/13-product.tex#L2078-L2088) · [Exercise, lines 2389–2396](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/13-product.tex#L2389-L2396)

7. **The Fredholm converse omits one localization estimate.** A kernel with left point outside the packet chart can still be singular along $M_\phi^2$. For a packet with nonzero fibre covector, the localized off-diagonal kernel is smooth in the right fibre variable with symbol estimates in the base. Integrate by parts there against the fibre oscillation; taking enough derivatives compensates for the packet's $t^{1/2}$ derivatives. This makes the omitted part $o(1)$ in $L^2$. The whole-fibre base packet and uniform lower bounds then give the stated necessity. [Proof, lines 2237–2259](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/13-product.tex#L2237-L2259)

8. **The tensor-product adjoint proof uses compatible densities.** To get the displayed formula for $A_\nu^*$, take the total density to be the given fibre density times the pullback of the density used for $A^*$ on the base. This is an available choice and the index conclusion is valid. The paragraph afterwards should attribute full ellipticity to both the fibre ellipticity and the invertible base symbol of $A$. [Proof and discussion, lines 2286–2329](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/13-product.tex#L2286-L2329)
