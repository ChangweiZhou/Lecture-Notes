## Appendix A

- **A.1, lines 10–64.** Checked completeness in the operator norm, the $C^*$-identity, the Neumann series and the numerical-radius proof for self-adjoint operators. In the $C^*$-identity proof the middle inner product is real and nonnegative, so the inequality is valid for the complex Hilbert spaces used here. In the numerical-radius proof, the step $y=Tx/\|Tx\|$ is needed only for $Tx\ne0$.
- **A.2, lines 66–133.** Checked the compactness equivalences, finite-rank approximation by projections and the eigenvector construction. The converse of (b) uses weak sequential compactness of bounded sets in Hilbert space. It also follows by working in the closed separable span of the particular sequence and extracting a subsequence in coordinates. The compact spectral theorem handles an empty eigenvector list when $T=0$.
- **A.3, lines 135–274.** Checked the generalized inverse, the compact parametrix criterion, the index exact sequence, the perturbation formula and the proof that the generalized eigenspace stabilizes. Lines 143–145 use the closed-range theorem for the adjoint. Insert the short argument that $A:\ker(A)^\perp\to\operatorname{Ran}(A)$ is a bounded isomorphism and that its adjoint is also one. In A.3’s index stability theorem, write $\|G\|\|E\|<1$ to cover the zero generalized inverse on finite-dimensional spaces.
- **A.4, lines 276–361.** Checked the basis expansion for Hilbert–Schmidt operators and kernels, the trace estimate and the cyclicity and composition identities by absolute convergence. Lines 314–318 assert completeness and the singular-value formula for the trace norm without proof. They can be proved using the singular-value decomposition and a summable decomposition of a trace-norm Cauchy series into Hilbert–Schmidt products. The Schatten remark invokes the ideal inequalities and Lidskii’s theorem as external input.

## Appendix B

### Findings

1. **B.1, lines 63–70. The smoothness criterion needs continuity on the closed model.** On $[0,1)$, set $u=0$ on $(0,1)$ and $u(0)=1$. All derivatives of the interior restriction satisfy the bounds. The given function is discontinuous. State the result as existence of a unique smooth extension of the interior restriction, or assume its boundary values are the continuous extension.
2. **B.1, lines 169–183 and 203–208. Hypersurfaces are connected components.** If a factor in $H_1\times X_2$ is disconnected, take the components. The same qualification applies to ${\omega_i=0}$, which consists of two points on the closed half-circle $\mathbb S^{1,1}$. For the induced boundary of a p-submanifold, the global test $Y\not\subset H$ fails for mixed components. Take $Y=\{0,1\}\times\mathbb R\subset[0,\infty)\times\mathbb R$ and $H=\{x=0\}$. The first component lies in $H$ and has no boundary there.
3. **B.1, lines 219–223. The displayed parabola is transverse to the boundary.** The chart $(x_1,x_2-x_1^2)$ makes $x_2=x_1^2$ a p-submanifold. The intended tangency example is $x_1=x_2^2$.
4. **B.1, lines 242–258. The global product for a b-map can be undefined.** On a countable disjoint union of half-lines mapping by the identity coordinate to one half-line, each source hypersurface has exponent one. A defining function for an individual hypersurface may equal $2$ on all other components. Then the displayed product diverges there and the claimed independence of defining functions fails. Use the local monomial definition or choose defining functions equal to one away from a locally finite family of neighbourhoods.
5. **B.2, lines 303–332 and Exercise B.1. The constant vector field exception needs $n\ge2$.** In dimension one the radial blow-up is two half-lines and $\partial_z$ lifts to $\pm\partial_r$ smoothly, with a transverse value. The rotation exercise already implicitly needs $z_2$. State $n\ge2$ in the lemma's last sentence and the exercise. Parenthesize the full vector field in the sum at line 322. At lines 395–397, the ordinary point blow-up has $a=0,b=n,m=c=0$.
6. **B.3, lines 442–463 and 546–553. State the positive-codimension convention and qualify the pullback claim.** The definition allows $Y=X$, for which the spherical normal bundle is empty. Even in positive codimension the pullback is surjective for the trivial blow-up of a boundary hypersurface in B.3.2(ii). The discussion’s nonsurjectivity argument applies when the blow-down has a fibre with multiple points. The properness argument can explicitly cover a compact set by finitely many compact subsets lying in charts, then use the compact spherical fibres on those subsets.
7. **B.3, lines 496–505 and 526–536. The blow-down exponent depends on each front-face component.** For $Y=\{(0,0),(1,0)\}$ in $[0,\infty)\times\mathbb R$, the exponent of the pullback of $x$ at the face over $(0,0)$ is one and that over $(1,0)$ is zero. The given exponent is zero for the union. Use one front defining function and exponent per component, with the exponent determined by whether that component maps into $H$.
8. **B.4, lines 698–708 and 856–868. The side face can be disconnected and the adiabatic coefficient is normalized.** For an interval chart in dimension one the two signs of $x-y$ give separate side hypersurfaces. The smooth function in the adiabatic remark is $\delta^{\dim Z}K_{B_\delta}$. The definition in Chapter 6 supplies the raw factor $\delta^{-\dim Z}$. The semiclassical vanishing and converse in B.4.4–5 concern the normalized scalar coefficient and are sound. The manifold corollary should specify that $X$ has no boundary, as it does in the application. If $X$ has boundary, the diagonal centre need not be a p-submanifold.
9. **B.5, lines 876–921 and 960–965. The lift rule misses mixed components.** In $\mathbb R^2$, take $Y=\{(0,0)\}$ and $Z=\{(0,0),(1,0)\}$. They are joint and nested, but $Z\not\subset Y$ and $Z\setminus Y$ is not dense in $Z$, so the prescribed lift of $Z$ is undefined. Define the lift componentwise, using the full inverse image for contained components and closure of the lift elsewhere. In the proof of commutation, the $v$-model in line 944 still has its boundary coordinates.
10. **B.5, lines 981–1068. Minor coordinate and rank qualifications.** In line 1032 the displayed transition is the inverse of the map the paragraph first announces. It still proves the diffeomorphism. At rank one, $H_W$ or $H_V$ may have multiple components and should be called unions of boundary hypersurfaces. Both compactification identifications and the spherical quotient bundle formula check out.
11. **Exercise B.3 and solution, lines 1472–1478 and `S-B-blowup.tex` 70–83. The full inverse image is called the lift.** For $u=z_1^2-z_2^2$, the strict transform is the closure of the radial curves in the four null directions. The full inverse image is the union of the strict transform and the whole front face. The computation of the angular differential and transversality is correct. Apply the normal-crossings statement to the full inverse image and state the strict-transform result separately.

### Proof and exercise coverage

- **B.1.** The inward cone and dimension of its maximal linear subspace give invariance of corner codimension. Boundary hypersurfaces are locally coordinate hyperplanes because a single defining function cannot vanish on two such hyperplanes with nonzero differential. Taylor division establishes the smooth positive defining-function quotient and the b-vector-field equivalences.
- **B.2.** Checked the polar chain rule and the spanning of the angular directions by rotations. The lift of a coordinate change follows by the integral Taylor factorization of its normal component, invertibility of the normal differential, and construction for the inverse. The projective inverse uses $\theta_j=\pm1$, $r=s|\theta|$, and gives the stated Jacobian and vector-field formulas.
- **B.3.** Checked gluing, compatibility with normal differentials, the quadratic defining function, product charts, lifted vector-field generators, bundle radial coordinates and the collar when $Y$ is compact. The tubular proposition imports the earlier normal fibration theorem. The connected-centre calculation yields $r\omega_i$ or a tangential defining variable as stated.
- **B.4.** Checked the flat-extension estimates, the radial and projective Jacobians, the semiclassical coordinate inverse, rapid decay in $Z=\theta/\tau$, and the converse Taylor estimates on compactly supported projective charts. The manifold corollary uses a finite localization on the compact diagonal and the off-diagonal extension. The normalized adiabatic statement follows by applying the same calculation with fibre variables as parameters.
- **B.5.** The local lifts follow from the subsets of normal coordinates that vanish. Checked the two transversal projective factors and the common nested model $(|(u,v)|,(|u|,v)/|(u,v)|,u/|u|)$. Blowing up the intersection separates the strict lifts under the density assumptions. For the relative identification the radius is $1/|q|$; for the double identification the squared common denominator is $\rho_U^2+\rho_V^2-\rho_U^2\rho_V^2$. The two angular ratios separate points and directions on the corner face, and the two single-blow-up charts separate points in the other model.
- **B.6–B.7.** Checked projective coordinates from the tautological-line trivialization, radial resolution via its unit sphere bundle, and the cut-open description using $f$ as a normal coordinate. The orientability statement follows from $w_1(T\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}\oplus L)=(n+1)w_1(L)$. The fibration proof constructs tangential right inverses and uses the complete flow of cutoff b-vector fields. The b-differential follows by differentiating logarithms of monomials. The matrix for a boundary-face blow-down is invertible on b-tangent fibres, and the corner projection has the asserted two-component special fibre.
- **B.8.** Checked the dictionary against the constructions. In the b-double-space chart the lifted diagonal is (t=1,y=y'), and the left-factor b-vector fields differentiate these normal coordinates with invertible normal matrix. The scattering and zero calculi and the push-forward theorem are cited background.
- **B.1–B.8 exercises and solutions.** Checked the projective chain rule and density, cone slice, Morse factorization and angular Hessian, diagonal corner codimension, the two distinct limits in the noncommutation example, corner exponent matrix, half-disc differential and fibres, and the face correspondences for the octagon and in higher dimensions. The Morse and dimension-one findings are above. The b-double-space example should assume nonempty boundary when it claims the original diagonal fails to be a p-submanifold.
