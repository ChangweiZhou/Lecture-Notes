## Chapter 5, Pseudodifferential operators on manifolds

### Findings

- **Compatibility of smooth structures fails transitivity as defined.** The definition requires the literal union of two vector subspaces to be a vector subspace. On $\mathbb R$, take $\mathcal F_1=\operatorname{span}\{x,1\}$, $\mathcal F_2=\operatorname{span}\{x\}$, and $\mathcal F_3=\operatorname{span}\{x,x^2\}$. Each induces the standard smooth charts. The first two are compatible and the last two are compatible, but the first and last are not because their union omits $1+x^2$. The amendment discussed in Solution 5.1 fixes a different problem. Replace the union in the compatibility definition and its proof by $\mathcal F_1+\mathcal F_2$. The maximal compatible space is then the space of functions smooth in the induced atlas, as constructed in Solution 5.2. [Lines 59–77](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/04-manifolds.tex#L59).
- **Infinite-rank smoothing operators fail the claimed weak continuity.** With the weak dual topology set earlier, continuity for just the supremum seminorm would force the operator to vanish on the common kernel of finitely many distribution tests and hence have finite rank. The heat operator on $S^1$ has a smooth kernel and infinitely many nonzero Fourier eigenvalues. Solution 5.5 already correctly proves the weak sequential continuity and gives the finite-rank obstruction. Bring the chapter statement into agreement with the solution. [Lines 247–251 and 545–551](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/04-manifolds.tex#L545).
- **The global product of pulled-back pseudodifferential kernels does exist.** At the triple diagonal the two wavefront sets are contained in conormals of the forms $(\xi,-\xi,0)$ and $(0,\eta,-\eta)$. They cannot sum to zero unless $\xi=\eta=0$, so the wavefront criterion permits the product. The local proof can stay, with a corrected explanation that it supplies the required order and symbol analysis. [Lines 869–883](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/04-manifolds.tex#L869).
- **Invertibility of the multiplier does not imply Toeplitz ellipticity.** On a trivial rank-two bundle, let $P$ be multiplication by $\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ and $a$ swap the two coordinates. Then $a$ is invertible and $PaP=0$ on the nonzero projected space. The exact condition is invertibility of $pap|_V$. Invertible $a$ suffices if it commutes with $p$. [Lines 2838–2845](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/04-manifolds.tex#L2838).
- **The Hodge spectral paragraph assigns the decomposition to the wrong operator.** On the standard circle $D(e^{ix}(1+i\,dx))=e^{ix}(1+i\,dx)$, but neither degree component is a $D$-eigenvector. The finite-dimensional complexes are eigenspaces of $D^2$. The spectral paragraph also needs $k>0$. The order-zero elliptic complex $P_0=I$ has $\Delta_0=\Delta_1=I$, with infinite multiplicity on any positive-dimensional compact $X$ and nonzero $E$. [Lines 2379–2384 and 2417–2421](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/04-manifolds.tex#L2417).
- **Index constancy requires a connected parameter space.** Stability proves local constancy. For example, over a discrete two-point space the identity and the Hardy shift (extended by the identity on the complementary modes for the ordinary case) form a continuous elliptic family with indices $0$ and $-1$. [Lines 1661–1665](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/04-manifolds.tex#L1661) and [lines 2759–2761](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/04-manifolds.tex#L2759).
- **The inverse-eigenvalue proof contains an algebra error.** Replace $u=AGu/\mu=Au/\mu$ by $u=AGu=\mu Au$. Also qualify the assertion $|\lambda_j|\to\infty$ to the infinite-dimensional case. [Lines 1818–1824](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/04-manifolds.tex#L1818).
- **The quantization diagram places $q$ on the wrong domain.** The displayed construction acts on $S^m$ and satisfies $\sigma_mq(a)=[a]$. It does not descend to the quotient, since a representative of order $m-1$ generally quantizes to a nonzero operator. Draw $q$ from $S^m$, or separately choose a section of the quotient. [Lines 953–967 and 982–995](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/04-manifolds.tex#L982).
- **A product cutoff need not preserve the stated support.** If $K$ contains small neighborhoods of two separated diagonal points, a cutoff equal to one on both projections leaves cross regions between the neighborhoods. Use a smooth cutoff on the kernel variables supported in the prescribed neighborhood of $K$ and equal to one near $K$. The amplitude estimates justify it. [Lines 326–330](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/04-manifolds.tex#L326).
- **The converse in the local characterization uses a kernel before proving it exists.** The theorem starts with an arbitrary linear map. Use the good partition to decompose it as a finite sum of $\phi_aA\phi_b$. Disjoint terms have kernels by (i). Intersecting supports fit in a single chart, where one can choose $\psi=1$ on both supports and apply (ii). This constructs the kernel and closes the proof. The answer after the theorem establishes the need for (i) with a single finite cover. If every chart is tested and disconnected chart domains are permitted, pairs of points can instead be tested in the union of two disjoint charts. [Lines 591–600](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/04-manifolds.tex#L591).
- **Solution 5.13 reverses the density weight.** In the collar $s=|y|^{-1}$ one has $dy=s^{-n-1}ds\,d\omega$, so the smooth positive density is $\nu_0=\langle y\rangle^{-(n+1)}dy$. The conversion is $dy=\langle y\rangle^{n+1}\nu_0$ and $\tilde K=\langle y\rangle^{n+1}K$. Multiplication by this weight still preserves Schwartz space. [Lines 756–763](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/S-04-manifolds.tex#L756).

## Chapter 7, The isotropic and scattering calculi

### Findings

- **The concluding metaplectic invariance assertion is false for the scattering calculus.** Fourier conjugation already interchanges the two scattering orders. More strongly, the shear $S(x,\xi)=(x+\xi,\xi)$ conjugates multiplication by nonzero $f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$ to an operator with Weyl symbol $f(x+\xi)$ and left symbol $g(x+\xi)$, where $\widehat g(t)=e^{it^2/2}\widehat f(t)$. The formula follows by quantizing $e^{it(x+\xi)}$ and superposing. The left symbol satisfies no $S^{m,l}$ bounds, since for any $j>m+l$ one can fix $x+\xi=s$ with $g^{(j)}(s)\ne0$ and let $|x|\to\infty$. Restrict the blanket assertion to the isotropic calculus. The isotropic principal symbol formula also needs the degree-$m$ homogeneous extension before pullback, or the factor $|Sw|^m$ and angular argument $Sw/|Sw|$ on the sphere. [Lines 2782–2797](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/05-isotropic.tex#L2782).
- **The complex-order notation changes the class at real orders.** The first definition uses all of $\Gamma^m$. The complex-order definition uses polyhomogeneous symbols, including when the parameter is real. Use $\Psi_{\mathrm{iso,ph}}^z$ here as in Chapter 8. [Lines 373–380 and 2841–2850](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/05-isotropic.tex#L2841).

## Chapter 8, Traces, determinants and the index

### Findings

- **Fixed-order ellipticity is stronger than invertibility in the union of filtered quotients.** The claim at [lines 750–761](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/06-determinants.tex#L750-L761) fails for $A=\operatorname{diag}(H,I)$, with $H$ the positive harmonic oscillator. It has exact order 2 and inverse $\operatorname{diag}(H^{-1},I)$ in the union, but its order-2 principal symbol $\operatorname{diag}(|Z|^2,0)$ is singular. Require the quotient inverse to lie in $\mathcal A^{-m}$ when testing ellipticity of order $m$. The commutator proof of the index formula extends to operators invertible modulo smoothing in the larger sense.
- **The determinant line gives one obstruction to an invertible smoothing perturbation.** [Lines 1274–1287](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/06-determinants.tex#L1274-L1287) say the obstruction becomes the first Chern class. For a concrete counterexample to sufficiency, let $P_y$ be the rank-two quaternionic Hopf projection on $S^4$, and let $T$ be an elliptic isotropic order-zero operator of index $+1$. One can take the one-dimensional oscillator annihilation operator times an invertible elliptic order $-1$ operator. Then $(T\otimes P_y+I\otimes(I-P_y))\oplus(T^*\otimes I_2)$ has fibrewise index zero and family index $[\operatorname{Ran}P]-[\mathbb C^2]\ne0$. Smoothing perturbations preserve the family index, so none makes this family invertible. Its determinant line is trivial since $H^2(S^4;\mathbb Z)=0$. The one-way implication in the determinant-bundle theorem remains valid.
- **The Hermite eigenfunction phase is inconsistent.** In [lines 133–139](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/06-determinants.tex#L133-L139), $D=-i\partial_x$ gives $(D+ix)e^{-x^2/2}=2ix e^{-x^2/2}$, so a positive normalizing constant does not give real $u_1$. Insert $(-i)^{|\alpha|}$ or use conjugated right factors in the Hermite kernel expansion at lines 212–220. The convergence result is unchanged.
- **Two introductory overstatements.** [Lines 11–15](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/06-determinants.tex#L11-L15) give trace and determinant as numbers for arbitrary finite-dimensional linear maps. These are numbers canonically for endomorphisms, while a map between equal-dimensional different spaces has a determinant as a map of determinant lines. [Lines 765–768](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/06-determinants.tex#L765-L768) omit the identically singular branch of the Fredholm alternative. The constant family $I-P$ for a rank-one smoothing projection $P$ has determinant identically zero. The numbered lemma states both alternatives correctly.
- **Triviality as a line bundle is weaker than multiplicative trivialization.** [Lines 1361–1369](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/06-determinants.tex#L1361-L1369) identify existence of a multiplicative determinant extension with ordinary bundle triviality. A character gives the trivialization in the later remark. A converse requires a trivialization compatible with multiplication, since arbitrary nowhere-zero sections can have a nontrivial multiplicative cocycle.

### Proof and formula review through line 1493

- **Setup and traces (1–386).** Verified the order and smoothness conventions by Taylor expansion at the radial boundary, uniformity of holomorphic Borel summation locally on parameter compacts, the sandwich estimates by Cauchy–Schwarz, and inversion via $(I+E)^{-1}=I-E+E(I+E)^{-1}E$. The Hermite approximation argument works with the phase correction above. For the trace, the phase-space integral converges for $m<-2n$, the cutoff argument allows loss of $\epsilon$ in each symbol order, and matrix units force uniqueness up to scalar. Absolute matrix coefficient summability supplies the trace-class assertion in the paragraph preceding the uniqueness proposition.
- **Fredholm determinant (387–674).** Verified path connectivity via the reciprocal nonzero eigenvalues, uniform convergence of compressed inverses via the Neumann bound, Jacobi's formula and exponential path independence, multiplication and conjugation by residual trace cyclicity, convergence of the log in Schwartz seminorms via the sandwich estimate, and extension at singular operators by finite-dimensional triangular reduction.
- **Index, Fredholm theory and spectrum (675–938).** Verified the sign of the trace of the parametrix commutator, the finite rank corrections by multiplication of the two proposed inverses, discreteness by the determinant identity theorem, and the finite rank Laurent coefficients by Taylor expansion of rank-one factorizations. The resolvent expansion gives $P=-\operatorname{Res}(A-\lambda)^{-1}$; the action on the generalized kernel and the recurrence on Laurent coefficients verify its range and nilpotence order. The quotient distinction and introductory wording are recorded above.
- **Residue, derivation, and trace defect (939–1253).** Polar integration gives residue $-(2\pi)^{-n}$ times the order $-2n$ coefficient before the convention change. The divergence theorem cancels homogeneous derivatives in converting left to right or Weyl symbols. Regularizer change introduces the negative residue of $B(\dot A_2-\dot A_1)$. The graded-free trace-defect sign is positive with $D_AB=[\dot A,B]$ and the chapter's residue convention, yielding the displayed index sign. The uniqueness of the isotropic residue trace is stated without proof in the manuscript. See the independent verification below.
- **Variation and determinant line (1254–1493).** Checked Maurer–Cartan, the graded cyclic sign, $D\theta=d\varphi+\theta\varphi-\varphi\theta$, and $d\bar\alpha=-\beta+d\gamma$. The transition of the frame is $\det_{\mathrm{Fr}}(I+X)^{-1}$, and the connection form $-\bar\alpha$ changes by its logarithmic derivative, giving curvature $\beta-d\gamma$. The two overstatements about the determinant line are recorded above.

### Exercises and solutions

- **8.1 has a dimension-dependent example.** The corrected exercise statement uses operators of order $s$ and the proposed solution verifies the scale by reducing it to one elliptic test operator. The counterexample in [solution lines 22–26](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/S-06-determinants.tex#L22-L26) uses $\delta_0\in H^{-1}_{\mathrm{iso}}(\mathbb R^n)$ without a dimension restriction. Near its support the isotropic regularity condition agrees with the local ordinary $H^{-1}$ condition. For $n\ge2$, $\int_{\mathbb R^n}\langle\xi\rangle^{-2}\,d\xi=\infty$, so $\delta_0$ is not an example. Specify $n=1$.
- **8.2–8.6.** Weighted $L^2$ and ordinary Sobolev bounds justify the norm-compact cutoff proof. Negative-order compactness follows by factoring through the positive Sobolev space. The Neumann inverse construction retains finite rank, and combining left and right inverses modulo finite rank gives a single Fredholm parametrix. Direct use of $[D_j,x_k]=-i\delta_{jk}$ verifies both oscillator commutators and the ground-state and lowering arguments.
- **8.7–8.11.** Checked alternating multilinearity, Jacobi's formula, and uniqueness by an open neighbourhood in the image of the matrix exponential. The Maurer–Cartan calculation and concatenation check the period lattice. Change of bases acts by $Q^{-1}AP$ and preserves the coefficient class. The Fredholm necessity wave packets have centre $k^2(x_0,\xi_0)$ and enough uniform decay after dilating to use dominated convergence. The Koszul signs give $H-n+2\mathcal N$, yielding the kernel in degree zero and the two indices.
- **8.12–8.15.** Symbol change by derivatives, the asymptotic product formula, and compactness of the sphere give the essential-support assertions. Cutoffs subordinate to the sphere cover sum to a constant outside a compact set. The Sobolev perturbation proof checks mutually inverse bounded maps. For the distributional wavefront set, tested both inclusions via cutoffs with separated essential supports, and checked the empty-set implication with $\sum A_j^*A_j$.
- **8.16.** Hilbert–Schmidt factorization and weighted symbol seminorms give trace norm convergence. The finite-rank repair at singular points, trace norm derivative estimate, and Jacobi formula check the determinant extension. For functional calculus, conjugation by oscillator powers preserves all nonzero eigenvalues by Schwartz regularity; the resulting series estimates give rapid decay of the remainder's Hermite coefficients. The proof at lines 753–759 can apply the estimate to each product in the sum from (b), or use the standard trace-class factorization via the polar decomposition.

## Independent checks of cited and sketched claims

- **Chapter 5, positive spectral projection, lines 2600–2604.** Grubb states the positive spectral projection formula for classical elliptic self-adjoint operators, defining it to vanish on the kernel, and invokes Seeley's complex powers for the classical ellipticity of $|A|=(A^2)^{1/2}$. The broader symbol class used in the chapter's example lacks a homogeneous principal symbol in general. A Fourier multiplier on the circle with symbol $\langle\xi\rangle U(\log\langle\xi\rangle)\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)U(\log\langle\xi\rangle)^*$, for the planar rotation $U$, is elliptic and self-adjoint but has no homogeneous leading part. Either restrict this example to $\Psi^1_{\mathrm{ph}}$, or prove the broader statement and give its leading symbol as an order-zero class. [Grubb, *The sectorial projection defined from logarithms*, pp. 118–119](https://www.mscand.dk/article/download/15217/13212/34718).
- **Chapter 6, global two-parameter localization, lines 2195–2201.** For a compact fibration, the ordinary semiclassical blow-up creates a finite projective chart with difference variables $(w-w')/\epsilon$ and $W=(b-b')/\epsilon$ in fibred coordinates $(w,b)$. The lift of the fibre relation is $W=0$ there. Blowing up its product with $\delta=0$ gives $\tilde W=W/\delta$, which is exactly the second variable in the displayed model. Near the total diagonal a fibred chart change has difference variables
  $$
    Z=M_{ww}\frac{w-w'}{\epsilon}+\delta M_{wb}\tilde W,\qquad
    \tilde Z=M_{bb}\tilde W ,
  $$
  with smooth invertible diagonal blocks after shrinking supports. Thus products of rapid weights are equivalent and parameter derivatives are absorbed by rapid decay. At the semiclassical face the triangular change has mixing block $\delta D_b\Gamma$, so the rescaled bundle extends smoothly in $\delta$, with canonical splitting into vertical and pulled-back base tangent spaces at zero. Its dual carries the semiclassical symbol. Away from the total diagonal, at least one difference on the support is bounded below, so rapid decay absorbs arbitrary $\epsilon$-powers. The remaining normalized kernels are $\epsilon$-flat with the base difference still rescaled by $\epsilon\delta$, and are treated in pairs of fibre charts over a common base chart. Off the base relation the same argument gives flatness in both parameters. For composition, a finite partition localizes the three base points to a common base chart, with base-separated pieces controlled by rapid convolution. The same rescaled-variable convolution absorbs the $\epsilon$-flat terms from separated fibre supports. Restrictions at the two faces therefore give the stated multiplication, division by the defining parameters, and common corner symbol. Local smooth extensions and a partition of unity give global surjectivity onto the compatible pairs.
- **Chapter 7, metaplectic topology, lines 2767–2772.** The cited §18.5 proves unitary implementation up to a scalar phase (Theorem 18.5.9), but supplies neither the fundamental-group computation nor the strong-topology double cover. Rawnsley's circle-map construction gives the fundamental group and generator, and de Gosson gives the metaplectic covering group and local charts. The unitary subgroup topology can also be checked from local metaplectic representatives, using a continuous phase branch for the Gaussian integrals and strong continuity on a dense subspace. [Hörmander III, §18.5](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/24214/1/Lars%20H%C3%B6rmander.pdf), [Rawnsley, *On the universal covering group of the real symplectic group*](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/52529/1/WRAP_Rawnsley_covering-preprint.pdf), [de Gosson, *Maslov indices on the metaplectic group*](https://aif.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/aif.1223.pdf).
- **Chapter 8, eigenvalue product, lines 530–541.** The product is correct with algebraic multiplicities. Simon gives the product as Theorem 4.2, Lidskii's trace identity as Corollary 4.3, and explicitly notes that applying Lidskii to exterior powers also gives the product. Simon's determinant agrees with this chapter's through their common finite-rank truncation limit in trace norm. The attribution could say “the trace-class determinant product formula, which follows from Lidskii's theorem.” [Simon, *Notes on infinite determinants of Hilbert space operators*, §4](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0001870877900573).
- **Chapter 8, uniqueness of the isotropic residue trace, lines 1030–1046.** Lopes gives the Shubin version on the quotient by smoothing symbols. The full-algebra statement follows because smoothing symbols are also sums of exact coordinate derivatives in the classical algebra. In phase dimension $N=2n$, for a Schwartz symbol $r$, the smooth vector field $F(z)=z\int_0^1t^{N-1}r(tz)\,dt$ satisfies $\operatorname{div}F=r$. Writing $z=R\theta$ shows that $F$ is homogeneous of degree $1-N$ modulo a rapidly decreasing remainder. Both $x$- and $\xi$-derivatives are commutators in left quantization, with $D_j$ and $x_j$ respectively. Every trace therefore kills the residual ideal and descends to the quotient, where the one-dimensionality is the cited result. Equivalently, at degree $d\ne-N$ the homogeneous divergence primitive is $zf/(N+d)$, and at the critical degree the zero-residue condition gives a spherical divergence primitive. [Lopes, *The regularity of the eta function for the Shubin calculus*, Theorem 23](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.1206).
