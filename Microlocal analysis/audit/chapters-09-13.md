## Chapter 9 · Elliptic boundary problems

### Findings

- **The negative-side contour formula has the opposite sign.** The definition at 07-boundary.tex 945–953 gives $G^-=-i\operatorname{Res}_{\xi_-}$. Lemma 9.8.3(i) at 981 and the later convolution at 1088–1091 apply a second minus sign to that quantity. For $\rho=(1+\xi^2)^{-1}$, $G^-=\tfrac12e^x$ on $x<0$, and the inverse transform is $\tfrac12 e^{-|x|}$. Both piecewise formulas should use $H(x)G^+ + H(-x)G^-$. The proof already computes the lower residue with the correct sign. The independently computed negative-side principal symbol and positive-side Calderón calculation have the right sign.
- **Lemma 9.8.2 uses jets absent from its hypotheses.** The lemma at 886–902 assumes $\sigma\tau=g$ only on an arbitrary closed $K$, yet concludes symbol estimates for all base derivatives there. On $K=\{0\}$ in one dimension let $D=(1+x)D_x$, $g=\xi^2$, $\tau=(1+x)\xi$. An actual parametrix has leading term $(1+x)^{-1}/\xi$ and $\rho_1$ has leading term $(1+x)/\xi$. Their first $x$ derivatives differ by $-2/\xi$ at zero, contradicting $S^{-2}(K)$. Require jet equality on $K$, for example by taking $K$ to be the closure of its interior. The theorem on layer potentials applies to a half-space and meets that stronger condition.
- **The book’s Sobolev orthogonal projection is pseudodifferential.** The statement at 1831–1835 conflicts with the standard Sobolev inner product defined at 04-manifolds.tex 1434–1443. If $C_i$ are the localized chart maps, the Gram operator $Q_s=\sum C_i^*\langle D\rangle^{2s}C_i$ is positive elliptic of order $2s$ and invertible $H^s\to H^{-s}$. The $H^s$-adjoint $P^{*s}=Q_s^{-1}P^*Q_s$ is order zero. The orthogonalization identity proved in Solution 9.4 gives $P(P+P^{*s}-I)^{-1}$. The denominator is elliptic by the pointwise version of the same argument with the $q_s$-metric. This proves the $H^{-1/2}$ orthogonal projection is classical. The assertion needs a qualification for arbitrary equivalent Hilbert norms.
- **The smooth topology and collar proof assume second countability without stating it.** 07-boundary.tex 251–256 says a countable family of coordinate seminorms suffices, and S-07-boundary.tex 52–55 claims a proper smooth exhaustion exists on every paracompact manifold. For $X=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}[0,\infty)$ with $I$ uncountable, a compact set meets only finitely many components. Any countable choice of seminorms misses components, and the sublevel sets of a proper nonnegative exhaustion cannot cover $X$. The collar proof works on each connected component separately, and the seminorm sentence can specify second countability.
- **The boundary-condition discussion compares against the wrong projection.** At 1969–1970 the symbol of the boundary condition must be “as large as” the complement of the Calderón projector. The correct condition at 1982–1986 compares the rank of $\pi$ to $\operatorname{rank}p$ and requires $\pi|_{\operatorname{Ran}p}$ to be invertible. The disc already falsifies the complement rank heuristic on each sign of the boundary covector. The theorem and disc exercises use the correct condition.
- **Solution 9.3 overstates regularity in its introductory sentence.** $D^{-1}(f_c)$ is smooth up to both sides with common boundary trace, as proved. It need not be smooth across the interface when $f_c$ jumps, since applying $D$ would then yield a smooth right-hand side. Replace “continues smoothly across” at S-07-boundary.tex 183–184 with “has smooth one-sided restrictions with the same trace.”
- **The local boundary condition rank argument needs a nonzero tangential covector.** 07-boundary.tex 2056–2068 concludes that the bundle rank must be even. In dimension one the boundary is zero-dimensional, the cotangent condition is vacuous, and a rank-one example such as $D_x$ on an interval with one endpoint constrained is Fredholm. Specify $\dim X\ge2$ and a nonempty boundary. The disc case remains correct.

## Chapter 11 · The wave equation and Weyl asymptotics

### Findings

- **The wave kernels cannot be smooth into one fixed $H^{-M}$.** 09-wave.tex 433–435 and 650–653 claim $\mathcal C^\infty(t;H^{-M}(X\times X))$ for one sufficiently large $M$. On the circle the $2k$-th time derivative of the cosine kernel at zero has diagonal spectral coefficients of size $|j|^{2k}$, so its squared $H^{-M}$-norm contains a series comparable to $\sum |j|^{4k-2M}$, divergent for large $k$. Use smoothness into distributions, or $C^k(t;H^{-M_k})$ for each finite $k$. Only $k=2$ is needed for the comparison.
- **The eikonal Cauchy condition is not unique on the full open cylinder.** Lemma 11.4.3 specifies the initial phase only on $W$. In a flat one-dimensional chart with $W=(0,1)$ and $\eta>0$, both $\varphi_+(t,x,\eta)=\eta(x+t)$ and $\eta F(x+t)$ solve the equation and agree initially on $W$, where $F(s)=s+h(s)$, $h$ is zero for $s\le1$, smooth and nonzero just above 1, and $F'>0$. They differ in every open time cylinder. Use uniqueness as a germ or prescribe the data on a larger chart and then restrict. The characteristic construction already uses such an enlargement.
- **A diagonal wave-kernel return need not return the covector.** The discussion at 1080–1088 attributes the same-direction condition to the restriction $x=y$. Restriction only identifies the points. Integration eliminates contributions with nonzero residual spatial covector $\xi-\eta$. A straight line from $(0,a)$ to $(1,-a)$ in the flat Klein-bottle covering returns to the same point modulo the glide reflection but has different initial and returned directions when $a\ne0$. The Poisson-relation proof uses the correct push-forward condition.
- **The forward kernel wavefront proof leaves out two arguments.** Lemma 11.8.1 at 1098–1112 must use finite propagation to handle the wavefront set of the Heaviside factor away from $x=x'$ at $t=t'$. The other argument in the lemma only treats $|t-t'|<t_0$, while the last sentence asserts exclusion of zero-factor covectors globally. For $t>t'$ apply the propagation argument of Proposition 11.8.3 to $K_{\sin}$ as well, or use the two homogeneous wave equations to force $\tau^2=|\xi|^2=|\eta|^2$.
- **Solution 11.9 jumps from pointwise finite order to a uniform polynomial order.** A distribution supported at $0$ is a finite combination of delta derivatives for each $\omega$, which does not itself give a single $J$ or integrable coefficients in S-09-wave.tex 488–492. Either specify a smooth distribution-valued family, which has uniform order on the compact sphere, or specify weak integrability and justify the uniform bound by applying the closed graph theorem to $C^\infty([-1,1])\to L^1(\mathbb S^{n-1})$ on a fixed supported test-function space. Test with scaled cutoffs of $s^j/j!$ to exclude coefficients above the finite derivative bound. The ensuing parity argument is correct once this is supplied.
- The wave trace is the Fourier transform of the **evenized** spectral measure in the introductory discussion at 48–57. Solution 11.6 establishes a sharp bounded circle remainder, not an improvement on $O(1)$. The remainder equals $1$ at every positive spectral threshold. The noncompact Volterra sketch needs uniform bounds on the volumes of bounded-radius balls and appropriate spatial support of the error kernels, in place of the compact $\operatorname{vol}(X)$ bound.

### 12.6.3 states an incorrect equivalence at small truncations

[Theorem 12.6.3, source line 1490](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/11-index.tex#L1490) says “some (equivalently every) $r\ge r_0$.” Its proof gives every *sufficiently large* $r$. A counterexample exists with $X=S^5\subset\mathbb C^3$, $N=2$, $r_0=0$. Identify the two constant input modes plus one chosen mode at frequency $1$ with a three-dimensional block. On that block set $A_x(a,b,c)=cx$, and set $A_x=I$ on its orthogonal complement. Then $A_x-I$ is a smooth finite rank smoothing family, so $B=I-A$ supplies the desired invertible perturbation. Moreover $A_x^*A_x=I-\pi_0$, so the proof of Lemma 12.6.2 can use $Q=A^*$ and $R=\pi_0$; its criterion holds from $r=0$ onward. Nevertheless $F_0(x)=x^\perp$ in the three-dimensional block is nontrivial. Its unitary frame bundle is $U(3)\to S^5$, whose clutching class is the image of the generator of $\pi_5S^5$ under the connecting map to $\pi_4U(2)\cong\mathbb Z/2$. The map is onto since $\pi_4U(3)=0$, by stability and Bott periodicity. Additivity of $F_r$ under truncation explains why it becomes trivial after adding enough modes. Write “some (equivalently all sufficiently large) $r$.” [Bott's primary paper](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/bott4.pdf), [Püttmann and Rigas on the first nonstable homotopy groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0301192).

### Findings

- **Pointwise surjectivity of the full symbol map does not imply that a family lifts.** In [the answer after Theorem 13.8.9, source lines 1549–1557](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/12-bott.tex#L1549-L1557), take $X=S^2$ and $a=\beta([H]-[1])$, where $H$ is the Hopf line. The series with leading coefficient $a-I$ and zero higher coefficients is a smooth map into $\mathcal Q$, since $a$ has winding zero on every fiber. Any putative invertible lift can be compressed uniformly in the transverse $Y$ variables to a finite matrix family by the same argument as Step 1 of Theorem 13.8.6. Its leading loop is stably pointed homotopic to $a$, so its families index is the nonzero class $[H]-[1]$. Invertibility gives zero by Proposition 13.5.5, a contradiction. The theorem's pointwise surjectivity stands. The answer also switches from weak contractibility to contractibility without establishing it there. The final Notes' contractibility question can be settled by the corollary to Palais's Theorem 15: the group is open in the closed affine Fréchet subspace defined by normalization, hence is a metrizable manifold. [Palais, *Homotopy theory of infinite dimensional manifolds*](https://studyres.com/doc/4327981/homotopy-theory-of-infinite-dimensional-manifolds%3F).
- **Remark 13.6.3 switches the bundle labels used immediately before it.** [Source lines 985–988](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/12-bott.tex#L985-L988) says that if $\operatorname{Ran}\pi_\pm\cong E_\pm$, the loop $\pi_-z^{-1}+\pi_0+\pi_+z$ represents $-\beta([E_+]-[E_-])$. State explicitly that its $E_\pm$ label the exponent signs, or restate it using the figure's convention and a positive sign.
- **The example for finite rank smoothing approximation can fail.** [Recalled fact B2, source lines 100–105](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/12-bott.tex#L100-L105) suggests adjoining an arbitrary $V_0$ to the first $j$ Laplace eigensections. On the circle choose $V_0=\mathbb Cv$ with equal tiny Fourier coefficients on successive pairs $n_j\ll m_j\ll n_{j+1}$, choosing their sizes rapidly decreasing in $m_j$ and making each pair dominate the subsequent tail. Let $f$ have coefficient $n_j^{-j}$ at $n_j$ alone, with $m_j\gg n_j^{2j}$. Both $v$ and $f$ are smooth. At the spectral truncation just below $n_j$, the normalized remaining part of $v$ has coefficients comparable to one at both $n_j$ and $m_j$. The projection of $f$ onto the augmented space therefore has a Fourier coefficient at $m_j$ of size at least $c n_j^{-j}$. Its $H^1$-norm is unbounded. Choose all coefficients real positive so there are no cancellations. For the rank-one kernel $A=f\otimes f^*$, the compressed kernels are unbounded in a smoothing seminorm (their other factor's $L^2$-norm is bounded below). The existence claim holds after conjugating spectral projections by a fixed finite rank perturbation of the identity unitary mapping a finite spectral space to $V_0$.
- **The normal form contains more data than the stable loop class.** [Discussion at lines 602–610](https://github.com/ChangweiZhou/Lecture-Notes/blob/5c20ed12c559cbbbd6934f2821086a050979953b/Microlocal%20analysis/chapters/12-bott.tex#L602-L610) says the pair of bundles up to isomorphism is what a loop carries.
