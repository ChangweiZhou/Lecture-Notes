## Findings

- **Opening, lines 14–21.** The duality is reversed on line 21. The traces are the dual of degree-zero Hochschild homology in the algebraic complex. For the completed complex, use continuous traces for the immediate identification. The first product correction for left symbols is $\sum_j\partial_{\xi_j}a\,D_{x_j}b$. Its antisymmetrization is $-i\{a,b\}$, as the chapter correctly states later at lines 875–895.
- **§18.2, lines 278–289 and 426–433.** The leading term of a normalized chain is $k$-linear in the differences, in total order $k$. The alternation argument is for normalized cycles. The phrase “non-zero differential everywhere” is false for this commutative example because $b:C_1\to C_0$ vanishes. “The higher differentials in the chain complex need to be computed” or an explicit statement of their size suffices.
- **§18.3, lines 501–510.** The displayed definition of $M_k$ should have $k\ge1$, with the $k=0$ case defined separately as in the next sentence. Otherwise $D_0=X$ would force $M_0=0$. The proof uses the correct separate convention.
- **§18.9, lines 1370–1393, and Exercise 18.4 solution, lines 124–139.** The proof computes homology for the completed chain space. A continuous trace kills boundaries in that space by continuity and density. The claim for all algebraic traces requires the additional theorem that every symbol in the kernel of the residue functional is a finite sum of commutators. Cite a finite-commutator theorem separately or qualify the claim by continuity. A published proof for connected manifolds of dimension greater than one is available in [Lesch and Neira Jiménez](https://ems.press/journals/jncg/articles/4329). The same qualification applies to the closing sentence of the circle solution. The two Fourier-mode representatives and their trace values are correct.

## Proof coverage

- **§18.1.** Checked the ordinary and cyclic face identities, including the $j=k$ cases, their signs in $b^2$, and the extra degeneracy $b's+sb'=1$. - **§18.2.** The HKR cancellations and inverse antisymmetrization have the correct factorial. The Euler contraction argument in the variables $x-y,y$ divides by total degree, counting the form degree as well. The comparison map is compatible with the $x_i-y_i$ differential, and the Laurent resolution uses the non-zero-divisor $r-s$.
- **§18.3.** Checked the mixed-derivative coordinate invariance, surjectivity from coordinate differentials, the decomposition into normalized and contractible summands, and the homotopy sign $(-1)^j$ including the cyclic endpoint. The normalized boundary module proof is valid. In differences $y_j=z_j-z_{j-1}$, the leading jet of a cycle is alternating by polarization. The noncompact remark requires locally finite supports, as stated.
- **§18.4.** At every total order there are only finitely many multidegrees in each permitted sum. Checked the radial Laurent factor and the degree of $dr/r$. Eilenberg–Zilber and Künneth supply the external quasi-isomorphisms. The shuffle compatibility factor is $\binom{p+q}{p}/(p+q)!=1/(p!q!)$.
- **§18.5.** The global quantization and its bidifferential product are imported standard symbol calculus. With those as input, the filtered differential is well-defined componentwise. Associativity gives $b^2=0$ on simple tensors and continuity in each fixed degree extends the identities. The powers of $h$ follow by conjugation with the homogeneity dilation. The argument uses a formal expansion in $h$.
- **§18.6.** Checked the $E^1$ and $E^2$ degrees and the identities obtained from $b^2=0$. In the convergence proof, reducing the primitive of a boundary only uses the one-line support condition at degrees $q>k+1-n$. At the critical line, the leading boundary is a $d^1$-boundary. The injectivity and surjectivity corrections have degrees tending to minus infinity, so coefficientwise completeness suffices. There is no extension ambiguity because $E^2$ is supported at one filtration index per chain degree.

At degree zero the radial form $dr/r$ accounts for the shifted summand. The spectral line $p=k-n$, convergence, and residue cohomology computation agree.
