\begin{abstract}
Let \(X\) be a compact \(n\)-manifold with embedded boundary and let
\(E\to X\) be a complex vector bundle. We give an expository account of
the continuous Hochschild homology of the Benameur--Nistor Laurent
complete-symbol algebra of the b-groupoid,
\[
 \cA_{b,L}(X;E)
 =\cO(X)\left(
 \Psi^\infty(\Gb;E)/\Psi^{-\infty}(\Gb;E)\right),
\]
where \(\cO(X)\) consists of functions with finite Laurent singularities at
the boundary and the algebra carries the topologically filtered completion
of Benameur--Nistor. The result,
\[
 \HH_k^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA_{b,L}(X;E))
 \cong H_L^{2n-k}(\Sbstar\times S^1_\sigma;\C),
\]
is the b-groupoid specialization of the Benameur--Nistor theorem on
Laurent complete symbols. The circle \(S^1_\sigma\) records symbol order
and \(H_L^*\) is Laurent de Rham cohomology at the boundary. We give a
self-contained computation of the \(E^1\)- and \(E^2\)-pages of the order
spectral sequence and check the hypotheses for the b-groupoid; convergence
and the continuous Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg theorem are quoted. A
bidegree count shows that \(E^2\) is concentrated on one row, so the
spectral sequence degenerates and, given convergence, the displayed
isomorphism is canonical; only its splitting into four interior and
boundary summands involves choices. We also describe an alternative
\v Cech--de Rham organization, which depends on a quoted localization
theorem for Hochschild chains, and a heuristic Rees--Fedosov
reorganization that is not an independent proof. Finally we separate this
result from the Hochschild homology of an unreduced operator algebra,
which depends on the chosen residual ideal and completion.
\end{abstract}

\section{The precise statement}

The phrase \emph{the algebra generated by b-pseudodifferential operators} is
ambiguous. At least four choices affect Hochschild homology: the allowed
operator orders, the allowed powers of a boundary defining function, the
residual ideal, and the locally convex completion. We use the standard algebra
for which the homology has a finite-dimensional geometric description.
It is the b-groupoid specialization of the Laurent symbol algebras studied in
\cite{BNsymbols,BNfamilies}.

\emph{Status of the result.} Theorem~\ref{thm:main} below is the
b-groupoid case of the Benameur--Nistor theorem on Laurent complete symbols
\cite{BNsymbols,BNfamilies}; it is not a new theorem. What these notes add
is a self-contained computation of the \(E^1\)- and \(E^2\)-pages of the
order spectral sequence in the b-setting
(Sections~\ref{sec:graded}--\ref{sec:laurent}), an elementary proof that
the spectral sequence degenerates at \(E^2\) (Section~\ref{sec:collapse}),
and a check of the hypotheses for the b-groupoid. Two inputs are quoted
from Benameur--Nistor: convergence of the order spectral sequence for the
completed continuous Hochschild complex, and the continuous, globalized
Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg theorem identifying \(E^1\).

For one boundary hypersurface, the standard b-groupoid has underlying set
\[
 \Gb=X^\circ\times X^\circ
 \;\sqcup\;
 \bigsqcup_{H\in\pi_0(\partial X)}H\times H\times\mathbb R.
\]
On a boundary component, composition adds the last coordinate. Its smooth
structure is fixed by the normal ratio
\(t=\log(x/x')\), and its Lie algebroid is
\(A(\Gb)={}^bTX\). For corners, one uses the standard groupoid with an
\(\mathbb R^j\)-factor over each open codimension-\(j\) face
\cite{BNfamilies}.

Let \(\cO(X)\) be the ring generated by \(C^\infty(X)\) and
\(x_H^{-1}\) for all boundary hypersurfaces \(H\). Benameur and Nistor
define
\[
 \cA_{b,L}(X;E)
 :=\cO(X)\left(
 \Psi^\infty(\Gb;E)/\Psi^{-\infty}(\Gb;E)
 \right)
\]
with their topologically filtered topology and completed Hochschild
complex \cite{BNsymbols,BNfamilies}. Here \(\Psi^\infty(\Gb;E)\) denotes
the \(\Gb\)-pseudodifferential operators acting on sections of \(E\) (their
kernels are distributional sections over \(\Gb\) of the pull-backs of
\(E\) and \(E^*\) by the range and source maps); we write \(E\) throughout. The vector representation identifies
this with the usual properly supported Laurent b-complete symbols. In
particular, one may write its underlying filtered union heuristically as
\[
 \frac{\bigcup_{m,\ell}x^{-\ell}\Psi_b^m(X;E)}
      {\bigcup_\ell x^{-\ell}\Psi_b^{-\infty}(X;E)},
\]
but this quotient notation does not by itself specify the topology used in
the theorem.

Changing the defining function from \(x\) to \(x'=e^f x\) gives
\[
 (x')^{-\ell}=e^{-\ell f}x^{-\ell}.
\]
Hence the change only multiplies a Laurent generator by a smooth
invertible function, and the
algebra, its filtration, and the answer below are canonically independent of
this choice. For corners one allows an arbitrary finite Laurent monomial
\(\prod_Hx_H^{-\ell_H}\), one factor for each boundary hypersurface.

Set \(Y=\Sbstar=(\Tbstar\setminus0)/\mathbb R_+\) and
\(Z=Y|_{\partial X}\). Thus \(\partial Y=Z\). The symbol-order circle is
denoted \(S^1_\sigma\).

\begin{theorem}[Hochschild homology; b-groupoid case of
Benameur--Nistor]\label{thm:main}
Assume the Benameur--Nistor convergence theorem for the order spectral
sequence of \(\cA_{b,L}(X;E)\) (Section~\ref{sec:collapse}). Then for every
\(k\geq0\) the induced filtration of
\(\HH_k^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA_{b,L}(X;E))\) has exactly one nonzero graded
piece, and there is a canonical isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 \boxed{
 \HH_k^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA_{b,L}(X;E))
 \cong H_L^{2n-k}(Y\times S^1_\sigma;\C).}
\end{equation}
The answer is independent of \(E\). If \(X\) has one embedded boundary
hypersurface, then
\begin{align}\label{eq:expanded}
 \HH_k^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA_{b,L}(X;E))
 \cong{}& H^{2n-k}(Y)
 \oplus H^{2n-k-1}(Y) \notag\\
 &\oplus H^{2n-k-1}(Z)
 \oplus H^{2n-k-2}(Z),
\end{align}
where a cohomology group in negative degree is zero. In particular the
homology vanishes for \(k>2n\).
\end{theorem}

The isomorphism \eqref{eq:main} is canonical once convergence is granted;
the K\"unneth decomposition for \(S^1_\sigma\) is canonical as well. The
four-summand formula \eqref{eq:expanded} uses one choice: a splitting of
the Laurent face sequence (a collar, defining function, and cutoff;
Proposition~\ref{prop:face}). The subspace
\(H^{2n-k}(Y\times S^1_\sigma)\) and the boundary quotient
\(H^{2n-k-1}(Z\times S^1_\sigma)\) are canonical.

\begin{remark}[Comparison with Melrose--Nistor]\label{rem:MN}
Melrose and Nistor \cite{MN} compute the Hochschild and cyclic homology of
the algebra of \emph{cusp} pseudodifferential operators on a compact manifold
with boundary. This is the full operator algebra, including its residual
ideal, not a symbol quotient. They interpret the index as a Hochschild
\(1\)-cocycle and obtain an extension of the Atiyah--Patodi--Singer formula,
whose boundary term is an eta invariant of the suspended algebra of the
boundary. Their work does not treat the b-calculus. The present theorem
concerns only the Laurent symbol quotient of the b-calculus. The
b-calculus analogue of the Melrose--Nistor computation is not
addressed here: the Hochschild homology of the full operator algebra
\(\Psi_b^{\Z}(X;E)\) and the connecting map to the symbol algebra.
Section~\ref{sec:unreduced} explains why that problem depends on the
choice of residual ideal and completion.
\end{remark}

\section{Continuous chains and the two filtrations}

The topology is part of the theorem.  For a topologically filtered algebra
\(A\), Benameur--Nistor do \emph{not} take the completed projective tensor
power of the whole locally convex space: multiplication need not be jointly
continuous for that topology.  In their notation the degree-\(k\) chain
space is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BN-chains}
 C_k^{\mathrm{cont}}(A)
 :=\varinjlim_{N}
   \bigl(F^{N}_{N,N}A\bigr)^{\widehat\otimes_\pi(k+1)}.
\end{equation}
(Benameur--Nistor denote this chain space by \(H_k(A)\); we avoid that
notation, which suggests homology.) Here \(F^{N}_{N,N}A\) is the diagonal
cofinal family of filtration pieces of \cite{BNfamilies}; equivalently, one
first takes the projective completion in the decreasing symbol-order
direction and only then enlarges the support and Laurent bounds by an
inductive limit.  We apply this with \(A=\cA_{b,L}\).
Whenever a tensor product below carries no decoration it denotes an
elementary tensor in the dense filtered subcomplex of
\eqref{eq:BN-chains}, not an ordinary completed tensor power of the whole
algebra.

For a chain \(a_0\otimes\cdots\otimes a_k\), the Hochschild boundary is
\begin{align*}
 b(a_0\otimes\cdots\otimes a_k)
 ={}&\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^j
 a_0\otimes\cdots\otimes a_ja_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_k\\
 &+(-1)^ka_ka_0\otimes a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{k-1}.
\end{align*}
Multiplication is continuous on fixed filtration pieces, so \(b\) extends to
the completed complex and satisfies \(b^2=0\).

Laurent order and symbol order play different roles. The first is built
into \(\cO(X)\); symbol order produces the spectral sequence.  On every
fixed piece in \eqref{eq:BN-chains}, filter elementary tensors by
\[
 \operatorname{ord}(a_0)+\cdots+\operatorname{ord}(a_k)\leq p,
\]
take the projective closure there, and then pass through the same
projective/inductive limits.  Denote the resulting subspace by
\(\mathcal F_pC_k\).
Then \(b(\mathcal F_pC_k)\subseteq\mathcal F_pC_{k-1}\). Exhaustivity,
completeness, and the convergence hypotheses are those verified in the
Benameur--Nistor topologically filtered theorem. Borel quantization explains
asymptotic completeness of symbols, but it is not by itself a convergence
proof for an arbitrarily completed Hochschild complex.

\begin{discussion}
\textbf{Why divide by smoothing operators?}
The principal-symbol filtration sees an operator only through its full
asymptotic symbol. Smoothing kernels form the kernel of that passage. Keeping
them gives an extension problem, treated separately in
Section~\ref{sec:unreduced}; it is not a harmless change of notation.
\end{discussion}

\section{The b-cotangent geometry}

The Lie algebra of b-vector fields is
\[
 \mathcal V_b(X)
 =\{V\in C^\infty(X;TX):V\text{ is tangent to }\partial X\}.
\]
It is the space of smooth sections of a vector bundle \({}^bTX\to X\).
Near a boundary point, choose product coordinates
\[
 (x,y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}).
\]
Then
\[
 \mathcal V_b(X)
 =C^\infty(X)\text{-span}
 \{x\partial_x,\partial_{y_1},\ldots,\partial_{y_{n-1}}\}.
\]
The dual local frame of \(\Tbstar\) is
\[
 \frac{dx}{x},\quad dy_1,\ldots,dy_{n-1}.
\]
Thus a b-covector has the form
\[
 \xi\frac{dx}{x}+\eta\cdot dy.
\]

On \(\Tbstar\) the tautological b-one-form and its differential are
\[
 \alpha_b=\xi\frac{dx}{x}+\eta\cdot dy,
 \qquad
 \omega_b=d\alpha_b
 =d\xi\wedge\frac{dx}{x}+d\eta\wedge dy.
\]
The form \(\omega_b\) is nondegenerate as a b-two-form. Its inverse is the
b-Poisson tensor
\[
 \pi_b=x\partial_x\wedge\partial_\xi
       +\sum_j\partial_{y_j}\wedge\partial_{\eta_j}.
\]
This is the geometric bracket appearing in the first nonzero differential of
the Hochschild spectral sequence.

\begin{proposition}[Anchor bridge]\label{prop:anchor}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] The anchor \(a:{}^bTX\to TX\) becomes an isomorphism of Lie
algebroids after adjoining Laurent coefficients:
\[
 \cO(X)\otimes_{C^\infty(X)}\Gamma({}^bTX)
 \xrightarrow{\;a\;}
 \cO(X)\otimes_{C^\infty(X)}\Gamma(TX),
\]
an isomorphism of \(\cO(X)\)-modules preserving brackets.
\item[(ii)] There is a (noncanonical) isomorphism of smooth vector bundles
\({}^bTX\cong TX\). Consequently \(Y=\Sbstar\cong S^*X\) as sphere
bundles over \(X\), by a diffeomorphism carrying \(Z\) onto
\(S^*X|_{\partial X}\), and
\[
 H_L^*(\Sbstar\times S^1_\sigma)
 \cong H_L^*(S^*X\times S^1_\sigma).
\]
\end{enumerate}
The isomorphism in (ii) is not induced by the anchor: the dual anchor
\(T^*X\to\Tbstar\) does not identify the homogeneous symbol spaces.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Locally the anchor sends \(x\partial_x\) to the ordinary vector field
\(x\partial_x\) and fixes \(\partial_{y_j}\). Its localized inverse uses
the Laurent coefficient \(x^{-1}\):
\(\partial_x\mapsto x^{-1}(x\partial_x)\). The anchor of a Lie algebroid
preserves brackets, hence so does its Laurent localization.

(ii) In a collar \([0,\varepsilon)_x\times\partial X\) choose a smooth
function \(h(x)>0\) with \(h=1\) near \(x=0\) and \(h(x)=x\) for
\(x\geq\varepsilon/2\). Define \(\Phi:{}^bTX\to TX\) by
\(\Phi(x\partial_x)=h(x)\partial_x\), \(\Phi(\partial_{y_j})=\partial_{y_j}\)
in the collar, and \(\Phi=a\) outside it. Then \(\Phi\) is smooth and
fiberwise invertible, since \(h>0\) and \(a\) is invertible over
\(X^\circ\). Its inverse transpose induces a diffeomorphism of cosphere
bundles \(\Sbstar\to S^*X\) over the identity of \(X\), hence carries the
restriction to \(\partial X\) onto the restriction to \(\partial X\).
A boundary defining function of \(S^*X\) pulls back to one of \(Y\), so
the diffeomorphism identifies the Laurent de Rham complexes.

Why not more: in the coordinates above the dual anchor is
\(\xi\,dx+\eta\cdot dy\mapsto (x\xi)\,dx/x+\eta\cdot dy\), so it kills the
conormal direction at \(\partial X\) and does not map \(T^*X\setminus0\)
into \(\Tbstar\setminus0\). For instance, the homogeneous function
\(\xi_b/|\zeta_b|\) pulls back to \(x\xi/\sqrt{x^2\xi^2+|\eta|^2}\), which
is not Laurent-smooth near \(x=\eta=0\). So no chain-level isomorphism of
homogeneous Laurent symbol complexes is induced by the anchor.
\end{proof}

\section{The associated graded algebra}\label{sec:graded}

Filter \(\cA_{b,L}\) by operator order:
\[
 F_m\cA_{b,L}
 =\operatorname{image}\left(
 \bigcup_{\ell\geq0}x^{-\ell}\Psi_b^m(X;E)
 \right),
 \qquad m\in\Z.
\]
The filtration is exhaustive, separated in the complete-symbol sense, and
multiplicative. Principal symbols give
\[
 \gr_m\cA_{b,L}
 \cong \cO_L(\Tbstar\setminus0;\End E)_m,
\]
the Laurent functions that are homogeneous of degree \(m\) in the fibers.
In a b-Darboux chart, a choice of local Weyl quantization gives the
expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symbol-product}
 a\# c\sim ac+\frac{1}{2i}\{a,c\}_b
 +\text{terms two orders lower},
\end{equation}
where changing quantization changes the individual lower-order symmetric
terms. Without a chosen global symplectic connection, the full Weyl
formula is not coordinate invariant. What is invariant, and all that is
used below, is the commutator symbol
\[
 \sigma_{m+m'-1}([A,C])=i^{-1}\{a,c\}_b.
\]
This one-order drop is the origin of the first nonzero differential.

\begin{lemma}[Morita reduction]
The coefficient bundle \(E\) does not change the Hochschild homology.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The global module of complete symbols with coefficients in \(E\) is a
finitely generated projective equivalence bimodule between the scalar
algebra and its \(\operatorname{End}E\)-coefficient algebra. The associated
matrix-trace and corner maps are continuous and filtration preserving, and
the standard matrix-unit homotopy proves Morita invariance. This is a
global Morita equivalence; no partition of unity is used to glue local
chain homotopies. The topologically filtered version, including graded
bundles, is treated in
\cite{BNsymbols,BNfamilies}.
\end{proof}

We therefore suppress \(E\). The Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg map on the
associated graded algebra is
\[
 \chi(a_0\otimes\cdots\otimes a_j)
 =\frac1{j!}a_0\,da_1\wedge\cdots\wedge da_j.
\]

For \(w\in\Z\) let \(\Omega_L^j(\Tbstar\setminus0)_{(w)}\) be the Laurent
\(j\)-forms of fiber weight \(w\), that is, with
\(\mathcal L_{\mathcal E}\alpha=w\alpha\) for the radial (Euler) vector
field \(\mathcal E\), and put
\(\Omega_L^j(\Tbstar\setminus0)_{\mathrm{hom}}
=\bigoplus_{w}\Omega_L^j(\Tbstar\setminus0)_{(w)}\). If \(a_i\) is
homogeneous of degree \(m_i\), then \(a_0\,da_1\wedge\cdots\wedge da_j\)
has weight \(m_0+\cdots+m_j\), since \(d\) commutes with dilation.

\begin{proposition}[The \(E^1\)-page]\label{prop:E1}
The HKR map identifies the first page of the order spectral sequence with
homogeneous Laurent differential forms on \(\Tbstar\setminus0\), the fiber
weight being the total symbol order:
\[
 E^1_{p,q}\cong\Omega_L^{p+q}(\Tbstar\setminus0)_{(p)} .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On each b-cotangent chart the associated graded product is ordinary
multiplication of scalar homogeneous symbols. Antisymmetrization gives the
inverse to \(\chi\) on Hochschild homology:
\[
 a_0\,da_1\wedge\cdots\wedge da_j
 \longmapsto
 \sum_{\tau\in S_j}\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)
 a_0\otimes a_{\tau(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{\tau(j)}.
\]
The displayed antisymmetrization is not a literal chain-level inverse;
the two composites become inverse after the Koszul homotopy. For the
topologically filtered Laurent groupoid algebra, continuity, completion,
and globalization are the HKR proposition used by Benameur--Nistor
\cite{BNsymbols,BNfamilies}. It may be proved through the sheaf of local
Koszul resolutions and its fine resolution, but a bare partition of unity
does not patch chain homotopies. Fiber dilation commutes with the published
quasi-isomorphism, so it retains homogeneity. A chain of total order \(p\)
in Hochschild degree \(k=p+q\) is sent to a \(k\)-form of weight \(p\),
which is the displayed indexing.
\end{proof}

\section{The first differential is Poisson homology}\label{sec:poisson}

For classical b-symbols \(a\) and \(c\), symbolic composition gives
\[
 \sigma_{m+m'-1}([A,C])
 =\frac1{i}\{a,c\}_b.
\]
Consequently the first differential after HKR is, up to the harmless factor
\(i\), the Poisson differential
\[
 \delta_b=[\iota_{\pi_b},d]
 =\iota_{\pi_b}d-d\iota_{\pi_b}.
\]
Explicitly,
\begin{align}\label{eq:poisson-boundary}
 \delta_b(a_0\,da_1\wedge\cdots\wedge da_j)
 ={}&\sum_{r=1}^j(-1)^{r+1}\{a_0,a_r\}_b
       \,da_1\wedge\cdots\widehat{da_r}\cdots\wedge da_j\notag\\
 &+\sum_{1\leq r<s\leq j}(-1)^{r+s}a_0\,d\{a_r,a_s\}_b
       \wedge da_1\wedge\cdots
       \widehat{da_r}\cdots\widehat{da_s}\cdots\wedge da_j.
\end{align}
To see this differential directly, insert the order-one-lower part of
\eqref{eq:symbol-product} into each multiplication in the Hochschild
boundary. The symmetric terms cancel after HKR antisymmetrization, while the
commutator terms combine to \eqref{eq:poisson-boundary}.

Let \(N=2n\) be the dimension of \(\Tbstar\), put
\(\mu_b=\omega_b^n/n!\), and let
\(\pi_b^\sharp:\Omega^j\to\Lambda^jT\) be contraction of every covector
with \(\pi_b\). Symplectic duality is the map
\[
 *_b:\Omega_L^j(\Tbstar\setminus0)
 \longrightarrow
 \Omega_L^{N-j}(\Tbstar\setminus0),
 \qquad *_b\alpha=\iota_{\pi_b^\sharp\alpha}\mu_b,
\]
and \(*_b^2=1\) up to the standard degree sign. Applying the two sides to a
monomial in the Darboux b-frame
\[
 \frac{dx}{x},dy_1,\ldots,dy_{n-1},d\xi,d\eta_1,\ldots,d\eta_{n-1}
\]
gives, with the displayed convention for \(\delta_b\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symplectic-duality}
 *_b\delta_b=(-1)^{j+1}d*_b
 \quad\text{on }\Omega_L^j.
\end{equation}
The calculation remains valid at \(x=0\): the displayed frame is smooth as a
b-frame, and both operators preserve finite Laurent pole order. Therefore a
\(\delta_b\)-cycle of degree \(j\) is carried to a closed Laurent form of
degree \(2n-j\), while boundaries are carried to exact forms.

The operator \(*_b\) also shifts fiber weight. Since \(\alpha_b\) and hence
\(\omega_b\) have weight \(1\), \(\mu_b\) has weight \(n\) and \(\pi_b\) has
weight \(-1\). Contracting a \(j\)-form of weight \(w\) with \(\pi_b\) in
each slot gives a \(j\)-vector of weight \(w-j\), and contracting this
into \(\mu_b\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight-shift}
 *_b:\Omega_L^j(\Tbstar\setminus0)_{(w)}
 \xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}
 \Omega_L^{2n-j}(\Tbstar\setminus0)_{(w+n-j)} .
\end{equation}
In particular \(\delta_b\), which lowers both \(j\) and \(w\) by one,
preserves \(w-j\), consistent with \(d\) preserving weight.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:poisson}
On the homogeneous complex of the \(E^1\)-page, Poisson homology is
Laurent de Rham cohomology with complementary degree and shifted weight:
\[
 H_j^{\pi_b,L}(\Tbstar\setminus0)_{(w)}
 \cong H_L^{2n-j}(\Tbstar\setminus0)_{(w+n-j)},
 \qquad
 H_j^{\pi_b,L}(\Tbstar\setminus0)_{\mathrm{hom}}
 \cong H_L^{2n-j}(\Tbstar\setminus0)_{\mathrm{hom}} .
\]
\end{corollary}

\section{Homogeneity and the symbol-order circle}\label{sec:homogeneity}

Choose a positive fiber norm \(r\) on \(\Tbstar\setminus0\). Then
\[
 \Tbstar\setminus0\cong Y\times\mathbb R_+,
 \qquad (q,r)\longmapsto rq.
\]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:E1} and \eqref{eq:weight-shift}, the column
\(E^1_{p,\bullet}\) in Hochschild degree \(k\) corresponds under \(*_b\) to
\((2n-k)\)-forms of weight \(p+n-k\). This bookkeeping matters:
\(\delta_b\) lowers fiber homogeneity by one, whereas \(*_b\) changes both
form degree and homogeneity.

The zero-weight reduction is elementary. The Euler vector field
\(\mathcal E=r\partial_r\) is dilation invariant, so \(d\) and
\(\iota_{\mathcal E}\) preserve weight. If a form \(\alpha\) has nonzero
weight \(w\), Cartan's formula gives
\[
 (d\iota_{\mathcal E}+\iota_{\mathcal E}d)\alpha
 =\mathcal L_{\mathcal E}\alpha=w\alpha.
\]
Thus \(w^{-1}\iota_{\mathcal E}\) is a contracting homotopy of the
weight-\(w\) subcomplex, and only weight \(0\) contributes to
\(H_L^*(\Tbstar\setminus0)_{\mathrm{hom}}\). By \eqref{eq:weight-shift}, in
Hochschild degree \(k\) the only surviving column is \(p=k-n\), that is,
\(q=n\) (Section~\ref{sec:collapse}).

At weight zero, restriction to \(r=1\) gives a unique splitting
\[
 \alpha(r,q)=\alpha_0(q)+\frac{dr}{r}\wedge\alpha_1(q).
\]
Since \(d(dr/r)=0\), its differential is
\[
 d\alpha=d_Y\alpha_0-\frac{dr}{r}\wedge d_Y\alpha_1.
\]
Thus the weight-zero complex is the direct sum of the de Rham complex of
\(Y\) and a copy shifted by one degree. The complete-symbol order is an
integer grading with no preferred logarithm; cohomologically this two-term
complex is canonically written as the de Rham complex of an auxiliary circle:
\[
 \alpha_0+\frac{dr}{r}\wedge\alpha_1
 \longleftrightarrow
 \alpha_0+d\theta_\sigma\wedge\alpha_1.
\]
The formal order variable \(dr/r\) is represented cohomologically by the
generator \(d\theta_\sigma\) of a circle. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:order-circle}
 H_L^j(\Tbstar\setminus0)_{\mathrm{hom}=0}
 \cong H_L^j(Y\times S^1_\sigma).
\end{equation}
This is the source of the extra \(S^1\) in the answer; it is not a geometric
circle in the fibers of \(\Tbstar\), and no choice of fiber norm affects its
cohomology class.

\section{Laurent cohomology and the boundary face}\label{sec:laurent}

Let \(V\) be a compact manifold with one embedded boundary hypersurface and
defining function \(x\). The Laurent de Rham complex is
\[
 \Omega_L^j(V)=\bigcup_{\ell\geq0}x^{-\ell}\Omega^j(V),
 \qquad d:\Omega_L^j(V)\to\Omega_L^{j+1}(V).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Face decomposition]\label{prop:face}
There is a split short exact sequence
\[
 0\to H^j(V)\to H_L^j(V)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Res}}
 H^{j-1}(\partial V)\to0,
\]
hence an isomorphism
\(H_L^j(V)\cong H^j(V)\oplus H^{j-1}(\partial V)\).
A splitting sends \([\beta]\) to the class of
\((-1)^{j-1}\widetilde\beta\wedge d(\chi\log x)\), where \(\beta\) is a
closed \((j-1)\)-form on \(\partial V\), \(\widetilde\beta\) is a collar
extension, and \(\chi\) is a cutoff supported in the collar and equal to
one near the boundary.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Work in a collar and write a Laurent form uniquely as
\[
 \alpha(x)+\frac{dx}{x}\wedge\beta(x),
\]
where the tangential forms \(\alpha(x),\beta(x)\) are Laurent families with
a finite principal part and a smooth Taylor tail. Filter the collar complex by
pole order and then by normal jet. On its associated graded, a term has a
definite normal weight \(q\). For \(q\ne0\), Cartan's identity gives the
contracting homotopy
\[
 h_q=q^{-1}\iota_{x\partial_x},
 \qquad dh_q+h_qd=1.
\]
Induction over the finite negative weights removes the principal part;
the remaining positive Taylor tail is smooth and belongs to the ordinary
de Rham complex. This jet-filtration argument, rather than freezing all
coefficients at \(x=0\), is the Laurent Poincar\'e lemma. To globalize,
multiply the collar homotopies by a cutoff equal to one near \(\partial V\);
the error terms contain a derivative of the cutoff, are supported away from
\(\partial V\), and are therefore smooth. The only new normal class is the
weight-zero generator \(dx/x\).

In particular, after writing
\[
 \alpha=\alpha_0+\frac{dx}{x}\wedge\beta_0
          +\text{terms of nonzero normal weight},
\]
the residue is
\[
 \operatorname{Res}_{\partial V}:\Omega_L^j(V)\longrightarrow
 \Omega^{j-1}(\partial V),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Res}_{\partial V}(\alpha)=\beta_0|_{\partial V}.
\]
A direct calculation in the displayed order gives
\[
 \operatorname{Res}(d\alpha)=-d_{\partial V}\operatorname{Res}(\alpha).
\]
Thus residue is a chain map to the degree-shifted boundary complex. The
Laurent Poincar\'e lemma identifies its homotopy kernel with
\(\Omega^*(V)\), yielding the quasi-isomorphism
\[
 \Omega_L^*(V)\simeq
 \Omega^*(V)\oplus\Omega^{*-1}(\partial V).
\]

If \(\beta\) is closed of degree \(j-1\), let
\(p:[0,\varepsilon)_x\times\partial V\to\partial V\) be the collar
projection. Choose \(\chi\) with support in this collar and choose
\(\widetilde\beta=p^*\beta\) on an open set containing
\(\operatorname{supp}d(\chi\log x)\). Extend it arbitrarily beyond that
set. Then \(d\widetilde\beta=0\) everywhere that
\(d(\chi\log x)\) is nonzero, so the representative below is closed. This
collar construction gives the
cohomological splitting
\[
 \beta\longmapsto
 (-1)^{j-1}\widetilde\beta\wedge d(\chi\log x).
\]
Its residue is \(\beta\), because
\(\widetilde\beta\wedge dx/x=(-1)^{j-1}(dx/x)\wedge\widetilde\beta\) near
\(\partial V\). If \(x'=e^fx\), then
\(d\log x'=d\log x+df\); changing the defining function, collar, or cutoff
changes this splitting by an ordinary/exact correction. Hence the direct
sum is natural as a filtered extension but its explicit vector-space
splitting is not canonical.
\end{proof}

\begin{discussion}
\textbf{Where do higher poles go?}
A pole such as \(x^{-q-1}dx\), \(q>0\), looks more singular than
\(dx/x\), but it is \(-q^{-1}d(x^{-q})\). Higher Laurent coefficients occur
in the complex and are essential for closure under composition; they add no
new normal cohomology. Only the logarithmic coefficient survives.
\end{discussion}

Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:face} to \(V=Y\times S^1_\sigma\), whose boundary
is \(Z\times S^1_\sigma\). Then use
\[
 H^r(W\times S^1)
 \cong H^r(W)\oplus H^{r-1}(W).
\]
For \(r=2n-k\) this gives exactly the four summands in
\eqref{eq:expanded}.

For a manifold with corners, the same local argument has one generator
\(d\log x_H\) for every boundary hypersurface \(H\) through a point. If
\(\mathcal F_j(V)\) denotes the codimension-\(j\) faces, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corners}
 H_L^r(V)
 \cong\bigoplus_{j\geq0}
       \bigoplus_{F\in\mathcal F_j(V)}
       H^{r-j}(F;o(N_F)).
\end{equation}
Here \(o(N_F)\) is the orientation line of the conormal directions to
\(F\). If the hypersurfaces have been globally cooriented and ordered,
these lines are trivialized and the untwisted formula results.
Indeed, apply the one-face residue successively. Residues for distinct
hypersurfaces anticommute with the graded sign, so the summand attached to
\(F=H_1\cap\cdots\cap H_j\) is represented by
\[
 \widetilde\gamma\wedge d\log x_{H_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge d\log x_{H_j},
 \qquad [\gamma]\in H^{r-j}(F).
\]
The product of the nonzero-weight contractions gives an acyclic complement.
Changing the order of the hypersurfaces changes the orientation sign of
the exterior logarithmic factors; the local system \(o(N_F)\) records
exactly this ambiguity. Iterated collar extensions provide a noncanonical
splitting. This is only a sketch of \eqref{eq:corners}: the Laurent
Poincar\'e lemma above, applied one hypersurface at a time, reduces
\(\Omega_L^*(V)\) to the logarithmic (b-) de Rham complex, and
\eqref{eq:corners} is then the standard b-de Rham theorem for manifolds
with corners (for one boundary hypersurface see \cite{MelroseAPS}; the
corner case follows by iterating it over the boundary faces). The corner
formula is not used elsewhere in these notes.

\section{Collapse and assembly of the computation}\label{sec:collapse}

Combining HKR, the Poisson differential, symplectic duality, and homogeneity
gives the second page. The new point of this section is that the
degeneration of the spectral sequence is then a matter of bidegrees; the
only global input that remains is convergence.

\begin{proposition}[The \(E^2\)-page and degeneration]\label{prop:E2}
For the order spectral sequence of
\(C_\bullet^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA_{b,L})\),
\[
 E^2_{p,q}\cong
 \begin{cases}
 H_L^{2n-p-q}(Y\times S^1_\sigma), & q=n,\\
 0, & q\neq n.
 \end{cases}
\]
Consequently \(d^r=0\) for all \(r\geq2\) and \(E^2=E^r\) for every
\(r\geq2\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:E1}, \(E^1_{p,q}\) is the space of Laurent
\(k\)-forms of weight \(p\), \(k=p+q\), and \(d^1=i^{-1}\delta_b\) by
Section~\ref{sec:poisson}. By \eqref{eq:symplectic-duality} and
\eqref{eq:weight-shift}, \(*_b\) identifies \(E^2_{p,q}\) with the
cohomology of the Laurent de Rham complex in degree \(2n-k\) and weight
\(p+n-k=n-q\). By Section~\ref{sec:homogeneity}, nonzero weights are
acyclic, so \(E^2_{p,q}=0\) unless \(q=n\); for \(q=n\) the weight is zero
and \eqref{eq:order-circle} gives \(H_L^{2n-k}(Y\times S^1_\sigma)\).
The differential \(d^r\) maps \(E^r_{p,q}\) to \(E^r_{p-r,q+r-1}\). For
\(r\geq2\) the rows \(q\) and \(q+r-1\) are distinct, so at least one of
source and target vanishes, and \(d^r=0\).
\end{proof}

The degeneration is therefore a consequence of degree counting once the
weight bookkeeping \eqref{eq:weight-shift} is in place. What is not
automatic, and what we quote, is that the spectral sequence computes the
continuous Hochschild homology at all.

\begin{theorem}[Benameur--Nistor convergence theorem; quoted]
\label{thm:BN-conv}
For the topologically filtered Laurent complete-symbol algebra of a
differential groupoid satisfying the hypotheses of
\cite{BNsymbols,BNfamilies}, the continuous HKR map identifies the
\(E^1\)-page as in Proposition~\ref{prop:E1}, and the order spectral
sequence of the continuous Hochschild complex converges to
\(\HH_*^{\mathrm{cont}}\): the induced filtration
\(\mathcal F_p\HH_k\) (the image of the homology of \(\mathcal F_pC_k\)) is
exhaustive and Hausdorff, and
\(E^\infty_{p,q}\cong\mathcal F_p\HH_{p+q}/\mathcal F_{p-1}\HH_{p+q}\).
\end{theorem}

Benameur and Nistor also prove degeneration at \(E^2\); in the present
setting this is Proposition~\ref{prop:E2} and need not be quoted.

\begin{proposition}[Source dictionary]\label{prop:hypotheses}
The algebra \(\cA_{b,L}(X;E)\) is the one-face b-groupoid specialization of
the algebras treated by Benameur--Nistor.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The dictionary has six entries.

\emph{Groupoid.} The differential groupoid is \(\Gb\), with unit space
\(X\) and Lie algebroid \({}^bTX\).

\emph{Laurent multipliers.} The coefficient ring is precisely
\(\cO(X)=C^\infty(X)[x_H^{-1}]\).

\emph{Algebra and topology.} The algebra is
\(\cO(X)(\Psi^\infty(\Gb;E)/\Psi^{-\infty}(\Gb;E))\), equipped with the
topologically filtered completion of \cite{BNfamilies}; its continuous
Hochschild complex is \eqref{eq:BN-chains}.

\emph{Anchor condition.} The relevant property of the Lie algebroid is
that its anchor becomes an isomorphism of Lie algebroids once the boundary
defining functions are inverted. For \({}^bTX\) this is
Proposition~\ref{prop:anchor}(i). We do not use, and do not claim, any
chain-level identification of b-symbols with ordinary symbols through the
anchor; see the end of the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:anchor}.

\emph{Local models and coefficients.} Locally near \(\partial X\) the
groupoid \(\Gb\) is the product of the pair groupoid of the boundary chart
with the b-groupoid of \([0,\varepsilon)\). This product structure is the
kind of local model their hypotheses require; we have not compared it with
their precise formulation beyond this. The global Morita lemma removes the
coefficient bundle \(E\).

\emph{Spectral sequence.} Their filtered HKR theorem identifies \(E^1\)
(Proposition~\ref{prop:E1}) and their convergence theorem identifies
\(E^\infty\) with the graded continuous Hochschild homology
(Theorem~\ref{thm:BN-conv}). The \(E^2\)-page and the degeneration are
computed independently here (Proposition~\ref{prop:E2}).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:E2}, \(E^\infty_{p,q}=E^2_{p,q}\) vanishes unless
\(q=n\). Fix \(k\). By Theorem~\ref{thm:BN-conv},
\(\mathcal F_p\HH_k/\mathcal F_{p-1}\HH_k=0\) for \(p\neq k-n\). Hence
\(\mathcal F_p\HH_k=\mathcal F_{k-n}\HH_k\) for all \(p\geq k-n\), and
exhaustiveness gives \(\mathcal F_{k-n}\HH_k=\HH_k\). Likewise
\(\mathcal F_p\HH_k=\mathcal F_{k-n-1}\HH_k\) for all \(p\leq k-n-1\), and
the Hausdorff property gives \(\mathcal F_{k-n-1}\HH_k=0\). Therefore
\[
 \HH_k^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA_{b,L})
 =\mathcal F_{k-n}\HH_k
 \cong E^\infty_{k-n,n}=E^2_{k-n,n}
 \cong H_L^{2n-k}(Y\times S^1_\sigma),
\]
with no choices involved: every map in this chain is canonical, and the
identification \eqref{eq:order-circle} does not depend on the fiber norm.
A Borel quantization, which splits the symbol filtration on the algebra
only noncanonically, is not needed. Finally,
Proposition~\ref{prop:face} and K\"unneth for \(S^1_\sigma\) give the four
summands in \eqref{eq:expanded}; here the choice of collar splitting
enters.
\end{proof}

\begin{discussion}
\textbf{Why is convergence not automatic?}
For a merely algebraic union of symbol orders, an infinite correction can
escape the complex and an \(E^\infty\)-class need not lift to a chain.
Borel summation and the strict Laurent inductive limit are ingredients in
the topologically filtered axioms: the former realizes asymptotic symbols
and the latter keeps every individual chain at finite pole order.  They do
not by themselves prove convergence.  Convergence to Hochschild homology is
the quoted Theorem~\ref{thm:BN-conv}. The bidegree argument of
Proposition~\ref{prop:E2} shows that no higher differentials occur, but by
itself says nothing about whether \(E^\infty\) is the associated graded of
\(\HH_*^{\mathrm{cont}}\).
\end{discussion}

\section{A \v Cech--de Rham route (conditional)}\label{sec:cech}

This section sketches an alternative organization of the proof, which
replaces the global HKR assertion by a local calculation followed by
descent. It is \emph{not} self-contained: it depends on a quoted
localization theorem for continuous Hochschild chains
(Lemma~\ref{lem:cech-descent}), and it still uses the Benameur--Nistor
convergence theorem; \v Cech descent alone computes the \(E^2\)-page, not
the abutment.

Choose a finite good cover \(\mathcal U=\{U_i\}\) of
\(Y=\Sbstar\).  Near \(Z=\partial Y\), take product sets
\([0,\varepsilon)_x\times V_i\), and require every nonempty intersection to
be contractible and collar adapted.  Write
\(\widetilde U_I=U_I\times\mathbb R_+\) for the corresponding conic open set
in \(\Tbstar\setminus0\).

Complete-symbol composition is local in \(\Tbstar\setminus0\), so for a
conic open set \(\widetilde U\) there is an algebra
\(\cA_{b,L}(\widetilde U)\) of Laurent complete symbols on
\(\widetilde U\), with restriction homomorphisms for
\(\widetilde U'\subset\widetilde U\). Let \(\mathcal C_q(U)\) be its
Benameur--Nistor completed degree-\(q\) chain space: one forms the diagonal
pieces \(F^N_{N,N}\) over \(\widetilde U\) first, takes their completed
projective tensor powers, and only then takes the strict inductive limit in
\(N\), as in \eqref{eq:BN-chains}. Restriction of every tensor factor makes
\(U\mapsto\mathcal C_q(U)\) a presheaf of filtered complexes.

Form the double complex
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cech-double}
 K^{p,q}=\check C^p(\mathcal U,\mathcal C_q),
 \qquad
 D=\delta_{\check C}+(-1)^p b.
\end{equation}
The total degree is \(q-p\), since \(b\) lowers Hochschild degree and the
\v Cech differential raises \(p\).

\begin{lemma}[Localization of continuous Hochschild chains; quoted
input]\label{lem:cech-descent}
The augmentation
\[
 C_\bullet^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA_{b,L})
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Tot}K^{\bullet,\bullet}
\]
is a filtration-preserving quasi-isomorphism, and it remains a
quasi-isomorphism after passing to any fixed order quotient.
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}[Status of Lemma~\ref{lem:cech-descent}]
The augmentation is \emph{not} a quasi-isomorphism row by row: a chain
\(a_0\otimes a_1\) whose factors are supported in disjoint open sets
restricts to zero on every \(\widetilde U_i\), although it is a nonzero
chain. Such chains are, however, far from the diagonal, and the needed
input is that continuous Hochschild chains localize to an arbitrarily small
neighbourhood of the diagonal: the subcomplex of \(\mathcal U\)-small
chains, all of whose factors are supported in a common \(\widetilde U_i\),
is a filtered deformation retract of the full complex, and on
\(\mathcal U\)-small chains the \v Cech resolution is exact by a partition
of unity. For the symbol algebra of a closed manifold, localization of
this kind underlies the computation of Brylinski--Getzler \cite{BG}; for
fine algebras it is
Teleman's localization of the Hochschild complex \cite{Teleman}. We assume
the corresponding statement for the topologically filtered Laurent algebra,
compatibly with the order filtration; we do not prove it here.
\end{remark}

Filter \eqref{eq:cech-double} by total symbol order. The associated graded
of the total complex is the total complex of
\(\check C^p(\mathcal U,\gr\mathcal C_q)\), on which multiplication is
commutative. Compute its homology by first taking Hochschild homology
in each \v Cech degree. The local HKR map and the local Koszul homotopy,
which commute with restriction, give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cech-E1}
 \check C^p\bigl(\mathcal U,
 \Omega_L^q(\Tbstar\setminus0)_{\mathrm{hom}}\bigr).
\end{equation}
This is an intermediate page, not yet \(E^1\) of the total complex: the
\v Cech differential remains. Each weight-\(w\) piece of the sheaf of
Laurent forms with a fixed pole bound is a module over \(C^\infty(Y)\),
hence fine, and the union over pole bounds is still \v Cech acyclic because
multiplication by a partition of unity on \(Y\) preserves pole bounds and
weight. Since the cover is finite, the \v Cech direction is bounded and
\eqref{eq:cech-E1} has \v Cech cohomology only in degree \(p=0\), equal to
the global forms. Hence
\[
 E^1(\operatorname{Tot}K)\cong\Omega_L^*(\Tbstar\setminus0)_{\mathrm{hom}},
\]
the same page as in Proposition~\ref{prop:E1}. The first order-lowering part
of the symbol product gives \(d^1=i^{-1}\delta_b\), exactly as in
\eqref{eq:poisson-boundary}.

For a closed manifold these are exactly the local filtered steps in the
Brylinski--Getzler calculation \cite{BG}: complete symbols pass to
homogeneous functions, HKR gives forms, and the first quantum correction is
the Poisson boundary.  The present descent argument adds the Laurent
normal-weight contraction, the residue complex at \(Z\), and the strict
topologically filtered completion required at the boundary.

\begin{proposition}[\v Cech computation of the second page]
Granting Lemma~\ref{lem:cech-descent}, the total filtered complex
\eqref{eq:cech-double} has
\[
 E^2_{p,q}\cong H_L^{2n-p-q}(Y\times S^1_\sigma;\C)\ \text{for }q=n,
 \qquad E^2_{p,q}=0\ \text{otherwise}.
\]
This identification is independent of the good cover and the partition of
unity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Symplectic duality \eqref{eq:symplectic-duality} converts the Poisson
differential to the Laurent de Rham differential with complementary degree,
with the weight shift \eqref{eq:weight-shift}. The Euler homotopy removes
nonzero fiber weights.  At weight zero a form is
\(\alpha_0+(dr/r)\wedge\alpha_1\), so
\eqref{eq:order-circle} identifies the remaining complex with Laurent forms
on \(Y\times S^1_\sigma\); the row \(q=n\) is singled out as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:E2}.  Refining the cover gives a common refinement, and
the two refinement maps are homotopic on the augmented \v Cech resolution.
This proves independence of all auxiliary choices.
\end{proof}

The boundary term can also be read directly in the descent complex.  One
must not write the generally false short exact sequence
\(0\to\Omega^*(Y)\to\Omega_L^*(Y)\to\Omega^{*-1}(Z)\to0\): a Laurent form
with zero residue can still have higher poles.  Instead let
\(\mathcal K^*=\ker(\operatorname{Res}_Z)\).  The nonzero normal-weight
contraction in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:face} gives a
quasi-isomorphism
\[
 \Omega^*(Y)\longrightarrow\mathcal K^*.
\]
The exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal K^*
 \longrightarrow\Omega_L^*(Y)
 \xrightarrow{\operatorname{Res}_Z}
 \bigl(\Omega^{*-1}(Z),-d_Z\bigr)\longrightarrow0
\]
therefore yields the derived decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cech-residue-derived}
 R\Gamma(Y;\Omega_L^*)
 \simeq R\Gamma(Y;\Omega^*)
 \oplus R\Gamma(Z;\Omega^{*-1}),
\end{equation}
after choosing a collar splitting.  This is precisely the interior/boundary
decomposition used in \eqref{eq:expanded}; it also shows why the splitting,
unlike the residue filtration, is not canonical.

Finally, Lemma~\ref{lem:cech-descent} identifies the abutment of the order
filtration on \eqref{eq:cech-double} with
\(\HH_*^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA_{b,L})\), compatibly with filtrations, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:BN-conv} applies to it; the proposition identifies its
second page, and the bidegree argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:E2} gives
\(E^2=E^\infty\). This recovers Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, conditionally on
the localization input. Descent replaces the globalization part of the
HKR step, but it does not manufacture convergence.

\section{A Rees--Fedosov reorganization}\label{sec:rees}

This section makes the deformation behind symbol composition explicit. It
is a heuristic reorganization of the computation, not an independent
proof: nothing in it is used in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, the
\(E^2\)-page already follows from
Sections~\ref{sec:graded}--\ref{sec:laurent}, and the formal Hochschild
homology computed below lacks the order circle. It has two logically
separate parts: a formal Lie-algebroid formality statement and a
finite-order Rees comparison with classical symbols, which is elementary.

This is also the deformation-theoretic refinement of the
Brylinski--Getzler filtration: their principal-symbol and Poisson
differentials are the first two coefficients of the Rees star product below
\cite{BG}.

\subsection{The Rees deformation}

The algebraic Rees algebra of the order filtration is
\[
 \mathcal R_F(\cA_{b,L})
 =\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb Z}t^mF_m\cA_{b,L}
 \subset \cA_{b,L}[t,t^{-1}].
\]
It records both specialization to the associated graded and the return to
complete symbols at \(t=1\).  Since
\([F_m,F_{m'}]\subset F_{m+m'-1}\), its commutator is divisible by the
Rees parameter in an associated-graded chart.  Equivalently, for a symbol
of fixed upper order
\(a\sim a_m+a_{m-1}+a_{m-2}+\cdots\), factor off the bookkeeping degree
\(t^m\) and encode the descending expansion by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:formal-Rees}
 a_m+\hbar a_{m-1}+\hbar^2a_{m-2}+\cdots .
\end{equation}
The power \(t^m\) records the absolute order.  After it is factored off in a
fixed sector, we write the same Rees parameter as \(\hbar\); its successive
powers record loss of order.  Thus \eqref{eq:formal-Rees} is an
\(\hbar\)-adic chart on one upper-order sector, not a global
\(\mathbb C[[\hbar]]\)-module structure on the union of all orders.

On Laurent functions on \(M=\Tbstar\setminus0\), full symbol composition in
this chart becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rees-star}
 a\star c=\sum_{j\geq0}\hbar^j C_j(a,c),
 \qquad C_0(a,c)=ac,
\end{equation}
where \(C_j\) is b-bidifferential and lowers the unscaled symbol order by
\(j\).  Its antisymmetric first term is
\[
 C_1(a,c)-C_1(c,a)=i^{-1}\{a,c\}_b,
\]
so \([a,c]_\star=(\hbar/i)\{a,c\}_b+O(\hbar^2)\).

The space \(M\) has the symplectic Lie algebroid \({}^bTM\) with symplectic
form \(\omega_b\).  Choose a torsion-free b-symplectic connection and use
the homogeneous cotangent Fedosov construction, so that positive fiber
dilation acts equivariantly.  The Fedosov Weyl bundle has a flat differential
of the form
\[
 D_F=-\delta+\nabla+\frac{i}{\hbar}\operatorname{ad}(r_F),
 \qquad D_F^2=0.
\]
Flat sections, transported to Laurent functions by the Fedosov--Taylor map,
should give a star product equivalent to \eqref{eq:Rees-star}.
Fedosov quantization of symplectic Lie algebroids is developed by
Nest--Tsygan \cite{NestTsygan}; \({}^bTM\) with \(\omega_b\) is a symplectic
Lie algebroid, but we have not checked the homogeneous version or the
comparison with the b-symbol product in detail.  Equivalent star products induce isomorphic Hochschild
complexes, so the choice of connection does not affect the answer.

\subsection{Hochschild-chain formality}

Calaque--Dolgushev--Halbout prove formality theorems for Hochschild chains
in the Lie-algebroid setting \cite{CDH}; Dolgushev's smooth-manifold
theorem is the corresponding prototype \cite{Dolgushev}.  Their chain
complexes are built from Lie-algebroid jets and polydifferential data; we
have not shown that they agree with the Laurent chains used here, so the
following is a heuristic.  Twisting their module quasi-isomorphism by the
Maurer--Cartan element defining \eqref{eq:Rees-star} would give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain-formality}
 \left(C_\bullet(\mathcal O_L(M)((\hbar)),\star),b_\star\right)
 \simeq
 \left(\Omega_L^\bullet(M)((\hbar)),
 \mathcal L_{\pi_\hbar}\right),
\end{equation}
where \(\pi_\hbar=\hbar\pi_b+O(\hbar^2)\) is the formal Poisson tensor and
\(\mathcal L_{\pi_\hbar}=[\iota_{\pi_\hbar},d]\).

The formal tensor \(\pi_\hbar\) is symplectic over \(\C((\hbar))\); let
\(\omega_\hbar\) be its inverse.  The formal symplectic star operator
\(*_{\omega_\hbar}\) satisfies the same Darboux calculation as
\eqref{eq:symplectic-duality} and converts
\(\mathcal L_{\pi_\hbar}\) to \(d\), up to an invertible scalar and the
degree sign.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:formal-HH}
 \HH_k^{\mathbb C((\hbar))}
 \bigl(\mathcal O_L(M)((\hbar)),\star\bigr)
 \cong H_L^{2n-k}(M;\C)((\hbar)).
\end{equation}
The formality maps are b-polydifferential.  Hence they preserve finite
Laurent pole order: locally \(x\partial_x(x^{-\ell})=-\ell x^{-\ell}\), and
tangential b-derivatives do not create a larger pole.

Equation~\eqref{eq:formal-HH} concerns the extended formal star algebra,
before restriction to classical homogeneous expansions and before Borel
realization.  It must not be read as the complete-symbol answer.  In
particular, \(H_L^*(M)\) itself has no order-circle factor: that factor
appears only after the homogeneous order complex is formed.

\begin{remark}[Where the order circle does, and does not, come from]
The complex in \eqref{eq:chain-formality} is relative to the base field
\(\C((\hbar))\).  Thus \(d\hbar=0\), and it is incorrect to insert an
additional class \(d\hbar/\hbar\) into \eqref{eq:formal-HH}.  The single
class giving \(S^1_\sigma\) is the radial zero-weight class \(dr/r\) in
Section~\ref{sec:homogeneity}.

One can instead compute absolute Hochschild homology over \(\C\) for the
Laurent extension of a Rees algebra.  Then a class \(d\log t\) can appear.
The Rees grading ties the exponent of \(t\) to fiber homogeneity, so on the
diagonal order complex \(d\log t\) and \(-d\log r\) describe the same order
direction.  After specialization at \(t=1\), only the radial class remains.
Either convention yields one order circle, never two.
\end{remark}

\subsection{From formal series to classical complete symbols}

Formal formality does not by itself compute the continuous homology of
\(\cA_{b,L}\).  What follows directly from the classical symbol calculus is
a comparison at every finite depth of the order filtration.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-order Rees comparison]
\label{prop:Borel-formality}
After fixing a homogeneous classical symbol map, for every
\(m\in\mathbb Z\) and \(N\geq0\), the first \(N+1\) symbol components give an
isomorphism, continuous at every fixed pole bound,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-Rees}
 F_m\cA_{b,L}/F_{m-N-1}\cA_{b,L}
 \cong
 \bigoplus_{j=0}^{N}\mathcal O_L(M)_{m-j}.
\end{equation}
Under \eqref{eq:finite-Rees}, operator composition is the star product
\eqref{eq:Rees-star} modulo \(\hbar^{N+1}\).  These isomorphisms are
compatible with truncation, completed projective tensor powers on each
diagonal piece, and the Hochschild boundary.  Consequently the Rees and
classical order spectral sequences have the same \(E^1\)-page and the same
Poisson differential \(d^1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
There are three points to check.

\emph{Algebraic realization.}
Taylor expansion in symbol order gives \eqref{eq:finite-Rees}; no infinite
summation is needed at finite \(N\).  The b-symbol composition formula shows
coefficient by coefficient that the two products agree modulo
\(\hbar^{N+1}\).  Its first antisymmetric correction is the b-Poisson
bracket, proving the assertion about \(d^1\).

\emph{Topology.}
For a fixed Laurent bound, both sides of \eqref{eq:finite-Rees} are finite
direct sums of nuclear Fr\'echet spaces.  Truncation and the coefficient maps
are continuous.  Taking completed projective tensor powers first and the
strict inductive limit over diagonal pieces afterward is exactly the order
of operations in \eqref{eq:BN-chains}; hence the comparison extends to the
finite quotients of the continuous Hochschild complex.

\emph{Inverse limit.}
Fix a pole bound \(\ell\). If \(x^{-\ell}A\in F_{m-N-1}\) with
\(A\in\Psi_b^m\), then the full symbol of \(A\) has order \(m-N-1\), so
\(x^{-\ell}\Psi_b^m\cap F_{m-N-1}=x^{-\ell}\Psi_b^{m-N-1}\). The b-symbol
Borel lemma realizes every compatible sequence of truncations at this pole
bound by a classical symbol, uniquely modulo smoothing; thus
\[
 \varprojlim_N x^{-\ell}\Psi_b^m/x^{-\ell}\Psi_b^{m-N-1}
 \cong x^{-\ell}\Psi_b^m/x^{-\ell}\Psi_b^{-\infty}.
\]
The statement is false without the pole bound: an element of
\(\varprojlim_NF_m/F_{m-N-1}\) may need pole orders \(\ell_N\to\infty\) as
\(N\to\infty\), and so need not come from \(F_m\). This asymptotic
completeness does not, by itself,
prove that Hochschild homology commutes with the projective/inductive limits;
that separate convergence assertion is exactly the topologically filtered
theorem quoted in Section~\ref{sec:collapse}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the Rees--Fedosov picture]\label{rem:rees-scope}
Formality would organize the local differential beyond its first Poisson
term, and Proposition~\ref{prop:Borel-formality} identifies the finite
classical jets to which such a calculation applies. On the homogeneous
Poisson complex the Euler contraction makes every nonzero weight acyclic,
and the zero-weight splitting
\(\Omega_L^j(Y)\oplus(dr/r)\wedge\Omega_L^{j-1}(Y)\) has cohomology
\(H_L^j(Y\times S^1_\sigma)\). This reproduces the \(E^2\)-page of
Proposition~\ref{prop:E2}, but only because those steps are the ones
already carried out in Sections~\ref{sec:poisson}--\ref{sec:homogeneity};
neither \eqref{eq:chain-formality} nor \eqref{eq:formal-HH} is used. The
formal answer \eqref{eq:formal-HH} is a different group (over
\(\C((\hbar))\), for all of \(M\), without the order circle), and nothing
here licenses specialization of formal Hochschild homology to continuous
Hochschild homology. The Rees picture is therefore a heuristic explanation
of where the Poisson differential comes from, not a second proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{remark}

\section{Checks and consequences}

\subsection{The closed-manifold case}

If \(\partial X=\varnothing\), Laurent cohomology is ordinary cohomology and
\(Z=\varnothing\). Formula \eqref{eq:expanded} reduces to
\[
 \HH_k^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA(X;E))
 \cong H^{2n-k}(S^*X)
 \oplus H^{2n-k-1}(S^*X),
\]
equivalently \(H^{2n-k}(S^*X\times S^1_\sigma)\). This is the
Brylinski--Getzler computation \cite{BG}.

\subsection{Degree zero and residue traces}

For the completed chain complex \eqref{eq:BN-chains}, degree-zero
Hochschild homology is
\[
 \HH_0^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA)
 =\cA/\operatorname{im}(b:C_1^{\mathrm{cont}}\to\cA).
\]
We abbreviate the image by \([\cA,\cA]_{\mathrm{cont}}\); it need not be
closed.  Its Hausdorffization is the different quotient
\[
 \HH_0^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA)_{\mathrm H}
 =\cA/\overline{[\cA,\cA]_{\mathrm{cont}}}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} computes the former group; by itself it does not
prove that the commutator image is closed (but see below for \(k=0\)).
Continuous traces factor through the Hausdorffization.

Now assume \(n\geq2\), \(X\) connected and \(\partial X\neq\varnothing\).
Then \(Y\) is connected, since its fibers \(S^{n-1}\) are connected, and
\(Z\) has one component over each component of \(\partial X\). The
manifold \(Y\) is orientable, being diffeomorphic to \(S^*X\)
(Proposition~\ref{prop:anchor}(ii)), a cooriented hypersurface in the
symplectic manifold \(T^*X\setminus0\); hence its boundary \(Z\) is a
closed orientable manifold of even dimension \(2n-2\). (It is not a contact
manifold, which would require odd dimension.) In \eqref{eq:expanded} with
\(k=0\), \(H^{2n}(Y)=0\) and \(H^{2n-1}(Z)=0\) for dimension reasons, and
\(H^{2n-1}(Y)=0\) because \(Y\) is a connected compact manifold with
nonempty boundary. Hence
\[
 \HH_0^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA_{b,L})\cong H^{2n-2}(Z)\cong\C^{\pi_0(\partial X)}.
\]
Continuous traces are continuous linear functionals vanishing on
\([\cA,\cA]_{\mathrm{cont}}\), so they form a space of dimension at most
\(|\pi_0(\partial X)|\). Conversely, the boundary-face residue functionals,
one for each component of \(\partial X\) (see \cite{BNfamilies}; compare
the residue traces of the cusp calculus in \cite{MN}), are
continuous traces; granting their linear independence, the space of
continuous traces has dimension exactly \(|\pi_0(\partial X)|\). In that
case \([\cA,\cA]_{\mathrm{cont}}\) is the joint kernel of finitely many
continuous functionals, so it is closed and
\(\HH_0^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA)=\HH_0^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA)_{\mathrm H}\). If
\(\partial X\) is connected, the trace is the b-residue trace up to scale.

\subsection{A product cylinder}

Let \(X=[0,1]\times N\), where \(N\) is closed of dimension \(n-1\), and put
\[
 W=S(\mathbb R\oplus T^*N).
\]
Then \(Y\simeq[0,1]\times W\) and \(Z=W\sqcup W\). Therefore
\[
 \HH_k^{\mathrm{cont}}(\cA_{b,L})
 \cong H^{2n-k}(W)
 \oplus H^{2n-k-1}(W)^{\oplus3}
 \oplus H^{2n-k-2}(W)^{\oplus2}.
\]
This example separates the order-circle contribution from the two boundary
faces.

\section{The unreduced operator algebra}\label{sec:unreduced}

Let
\[
 \PsiL(X;E)=\bigcup_{m\in\Z,\,\ell\geq0}x^{-\ell}\Psi_b^m(X;E),
 \qquad
 \Psi_{b,L}^{-\infty}(X;E)=\bigcup_{\ell\geq0}x^{-\ell}\Psi_b^{-\infty}(X;E),
\]
the Laurent b-operators of integral order and their residual ideal (an
algebra and a two-sided ideal, since conjugation by \(x^{\ell}\) preserves
each \(\Psi_b^m\)), each with a chosen topology. The unreduced operator
algebra fits into
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extension}
 0\longrightarrow\Psi_{b,L}^{-\infty}(X;E)
 \longrightarrow\PsiL(X;E)
 \longrightarrow\cA_{b,L}(X;E)
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The answer for \(\PsiL\) is not obtained by simply reusing
\eqref{eq:main}. It depends on the topology placed on the residual ideal and
on whether kernels are required to vanish rapidly at the side faces, merely
have polyhomogeneous expansions, or are completed in an operator norm.

For an H-unital residual ideal, excision gives the long exact sequence
\[
 \cdots\to
 \HH_k(\Psi_{b,L}^{-\infty})
 \to\HH_k(\PsiL)
 \to\HH_k(\cA_{b,L})
 \xrightarrow{\partial}
 \HH_{k-1}(\Psi_{b,L}^{-\infty})
 \to\cdots .
\]
Melrose and Nistor study the corresponding extension for the cusp calculus
\cite{MN}. There the connecting map evaluates the index cocycle and produces
an eta-invariant term. The analogous extension for the b-calculus is not
computed in this paper; see also Moroianu--Nistor \cite{MoroianuNistor} for
the relation between index and homology on manifolds with boundary.

Here H-unitality means that the completed bar complex of the nonunital
residual ideal is acyclic; this is the hypothesis that prevents an
additional excision defect. Continuous excision and H-unitality are
substantial theorems of Wodzicki type \cite{Wodzicki}. Even for a rapidly
decreasing nuclear smoothing algebra, H-unitality is not proved by a
one-line finite-rank approximation; it has to be checked (or quoted) for
the completion in use. Allowing
polyhomogeneous side-face terms changes both topology and residual
homology, so H-unitality must be verified anew for the chosen completion.

The Hochschild connecting morphism can be seen on a symbol cycle. Choose operator lifts
of its tensor factors, apply the Hochschild boundary upstairs, and observe
that the result lies in the residual ideal because its image vanishes in the
quotient. The resulting residual homology class is independent of the lifts.
This Hochschild map is not literally the Fredholm boundary map in
\(K\)-theory. Compatibility appears only after applying the Chern character
to cyclic homology and using excision in both theories. Boundary logarithmic
classes then pair with regularized cyclic cocycles; the eta term is an
analytic realization of such a pairing, not an element produced by the
Hochschild boundary alone.

Thus \eqref{eq:main} is the complete answer for Laurent complete symbols,
while \eqref{eq:extension}, together with the homology of the chosen residual
ideal, is the correct computation for a specified unreduced operator algebra.

\section{Relation to index theory}

An elliptic b-operator determines an invertible matrix over the
complete-symbol algebra. A Fredholm index additionally requires invertibility
of its normal family. Its \(K_1\)-class has a Chern character in cyclic
homology, which can pair with cyclic cocycles arising from the Hochschild
classes above. In this qualified sense, the ordinary residue class supplies
the interior symbolic term and a boundary logarithmic cocycle supplies the
indicial correction. The operator-extension boundary map is the Fredholm
index only under full ellipticity.

The work of Liu and Ma on differential \(K\)-theory and eta-invariant
localization supplies a geometric refinement of this pairing: differential
forms and eta data refine the topological \(K\)-class. It does not replace the
Hochschild spectral-sequence calculation \cite{LMlocalization}. The
logical division is therefore
\[
 \text{Melrose--Nistor (cusp calculus) and Benameur--Nistor: algebraic homology,}
\]
\[
 \text{Liu--Ma: differential-\(K\) and eta localization of index data.}
\]

Mazzeo--Melrose's fibred-boundary calculus gives complementary analytic
background for normal families, mapping properties, and Fredholm criteria
\cite{MazzeoMelrose}. It does not replace the Benameur--Nistor
topologically filtered Hochschild theorem. This distinction is useful when
transporting the present computation to edge or fibred-cusp settings.
